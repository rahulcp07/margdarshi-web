## Access-control and organisational decisions

### 1. Approve the office hierarchy and use of Section offices

**Question requiring confirmation:** Is the authoritative hierarchy exactly `Circle → Division → Subdivision → Section`, are all four levels mandatory, and will Section offices be created and used in production?

**Why this matters:** The current schema permits these office types, but current audited data has no Section row and does not enforce parent types or cycle safety. Office-derived project scope cannot be reliable until the hierarchy is authoritative.

**Available options:**

1. Use all four levels and require every branch to follow the exact hierarchy.
2. Use all four levels but allow a project or officer to stop at Division or Subdivision when no Section exists.
3. Do not use Section offices and define a three-level hierarchy.

**Codex recommendation:** Use the four-level hierarchy as the canonical model, while permitting temporarily incomplete branches during migration. Validate allowed parent pairs and cycles, and do not grant descendant scope through an invalid or unresolved edge.

**Affected areas:** `offices`, `parent_office_id`, office ancestry/descendency helper, `user_assignments`, `projects.implementing_office_id`, project-scope RLS, Administration, and hierarchy preflight/backfill steps.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Use the following canonical office hierarchy: Regional Office → Circle Office → Division Office → Subdivision Office → Section. The Regional Office is the office of the Chief Engineer, the Circle Office is the office of the Superintending Engineer, the Division Office is the office of the Executive Engineer, and the Subdivision Office is headed by the Deputy Engineer/Assistant Engineer. These are office levels, not separate additional hierarchy levels. A Section may be linked under the concerned Subdivision, and where a separate Section setup does not exist, the hierarchy may stop at the Subdivision level.
Each office may contain several sanctioned, deputed or functionally assigned posts under the Head of Office. MARGDARSHI shall separately record the office, designation/cadre, functional post, actual officer assignment, jurisdiction and permissions.
Personal Assistants and Project Officers:
In the Regional Office, two, three or such number of Personal Assistants as required may be deputed, ordinarily from the Executive Engineer level. Each Personal Assistant may be assigned one, two or such number of Circles as specifically ordered.
In Circle and Division Offices, Personal Assistants may be appointed or deputed, ordinarily from the Deputy Engineer/Assistant Engineer level.
Project Officers may be appointed from the Class-II engineering cadre, including Junior Engineer, Sectional Engineer, Assistant Engineer Grade-II or equivalent posts.
Normally, in a Division Office, one Project Officer shall be assigned for each Subdivision under that Division. In a Circle Office, one Project Officer shall normally be assigned for each Division under that Circle. In a Regional Office, each Project Officer may be assigned one, two or such number of Divisions as specifically ordered. Their access shall be limited to the actual assigned Subdivision, Division, Circle, project or subject, unless additional jurisdiction is expressly granted.
Audit and Accounts Establishment:
MARGDARSHI shall recognise audit and accounts personnel in the Division, Circle and Regional Offices. In the Division Office, the audit/accounts establishment shall be headed by the Divisional Accountant. One, two or such number of Auditors as sanctioned or deployed may work under the Divisional Accountant. Auditors may be assigned Subdivision-wise, project-wise, contract-wise or subject-wise. Their primary functions shall include scrutiny and review of project billing, estimates, tenders, contracts, accounts, expenditure, recoveries and related financial records.
Auditors posted in the Circle Office shall review the financial, tender, billing and account matters of the Divisions or projects specifically assigned to them. Auditors posted in the Regional Office shall review the Circles, Divisions, projects or subjects specifically assigned to them.
Personal Assistants, Project Officers, Divisional Accountants and Auditors shall be treated as functional posts within the concerned office and not as separate office-hierarchy levels. Access shall arise from the actual assignment, effective dates, office posting, designated function and approved permissions, not merely from designation or presence in a higher office.
A single officer may hold multiple, temporary or additional-charge assignments. Every assignment shall have an effective start date and, where applicable, an end date. Every action, review, instruction, objection, recommendation, approval or administrative change shall be audited against the user’s active office, functional post and assigned jurisdiction.
The Head of Office may have full oversight within the official jurisdiction of that office, while Personal Assistants, Project Officers and Auditors shall have access only to the Circles, Divisions, Subdivisions, projects, contracts or subjects specifically assigned to them. Auditors shall not receive general engineering, administrative or user-management powers unless separately authorised.

### 2. Define what a project's implementing office represents

**Question requiring confirmation:** Must `projects.implementing_office_id` identify the lowest office directly responsible for the project, the controlling Division, or another approved level?

**Why this matters:** Office-scope inheritance depends on the starting office. Different interpretations can overgrant a Circle or Division or omit Subdivision and Section officers.

**Available options:**

1. Store the lowest responsible office and derive all ancestors.
2. Store the controlling Division and manage lower access only through explicit user assignments.
3. Add separate owning, implementing, and controlling office relationships in a later schema design.

**Codex recommendation:** Treat the implementing office as the lowest responsible operational office and derive ancestor visibility through the approved hierarchy. If ownership and implementation are genuinely different, introduce separate explicit relationships rather than overloading one field.

**Affected areas:** `projects`, `offices`, `v_project_register`, office-scope resolver, project creation/editing, existing-project backfill, Dashboard, Project Register, and every project-dependent policy.

**Rahul’s decision:**
The `projects.implementing_office_id` shall identify the lowest operational office having primary day-to-day responsibility for implementation and monitoring of the project.

Normally, this shall be the concerned Subdivision Office. Where a formally recognised Section has been assigned independent responsibility for the project or a defined project portion, the Section Office may be recorded as the implementing office. Where no Subdivision or Section is responsible, the Division Office may be recorded directly.

The controlling Division, Circle and Regional Office shall not be entered in the same field merely for providing supervisory access. Their visibility and authority shall be derived automatically through the approved office hierarchy.

A project may extend across more than one Subdivision or Section. In such cases, one office shall be recorded as the primary implementing office, and the other concerned offices shall be added through separate project-jurisdiction or project-office assignments. Such assignments shall clearly record the assigned project, geographical or chainage scope, function, effective dates and level of access.

The office recorded as the implementing office shall not by itself give every employee of that office access to the project. Access shall be limited to the Head of Office and the officers, Project Officers, Personal Assistants, auditors or other users who are specifically assigned jurisdiction or an approved functional role for that project.

Superior offices shall receive inherited supervisory visibility according to the hierarchy, but operational actions such as issuing instructions, processing, verification, audit, approval or editing shall depend on the user’s active post, functional assignment and approved permission.

The project record shall therefore maintain:
- one primary implementing office;
- the controlling Division derived through hierarchy;
- additional participating offices through explicit project-jurisdiction assignments;
- assigned officers and functional posts;
- effective start and end dates; and
- specific view, instruction, processing, verification, audit and administrative permissions.

If the legal owner, administrative controlling office and operational implementing office are different, separate explicit relationships may be introduced in the schema instead of overloading `implementing_office_id`.

### 4. Approve designation-to-scope rules for the named engineering posts

**Question requiring confirmation:** Should access be assigned as follows: Junior Engineer, Sectional Engineer, and Assistant Engineer Grade-II by explicit project; Deputy Engineer by Subdivision; Executive Engineer by Division; and Superintending Engineer by Circle? What scope applies to higher officers?

**Why this matters:** These rules are the core of effective project scope and must be identical in RLS, direct routes, mutations, exports, and UI choices.

**Available options:**

1. Adopt the proposed mapping exactly and define higher officers separately.
2. Give all engineering posts office-descendant scope.
3. Make all posts project-specific.

**Codex recommendation:** Adopt the proposed mapping: project-specific scope for Junior Engineer, Sectional Engineer, and Assistant Engineer Grade-II; Subdivision scope for Deputy Engineer; Division scope for Executive Engineer; Circle scope for Superintending Engineer. Give higher officers only explicitly approved jurisdiction—not automatic platform-wide access.

**Affected areas:** canonical post catalogue, `user_assignments`, `project_user_access`, effective-project-scope helper, project and dependent-record RLS, Dashboard, Projects, Correspondence, Actions, Inspections, Calendar, Reports, Search, Notifications, and attachments.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt the proposed designation-to-scope mapping, subject to active office posting, effective assignment dates, approved jurisdiction and functional permissions. Designation alone shall not grant access.

The default engineering scope shall be:

- `JE` — Junior Engineer: only the projects, project portions, chainages, subjects or Sections explicitly assigned to the officer.
- `SECTIONAL_ENGINEER` — Sectional Engineer: only the projects, project portions, chainages, subjects or Sections explicitly assigned to the officer.
- `AE2` — Assistant Engineer Grade-II: only the projects, project portions, chainages, subjects or Sections explicitly assigned to the officer.
- `AE` — Assistant Engineer: scope shall be based on the actual sanctioned post and assignment. Where functioning as Head of Subdivision or holding equivalent responsibility, Subdivision scope may be granted; otherwise access shall be limited to explicitly assigned projects or subjects.
- `DE` — Deputy Engineer: all projects implemented by the officer’s assigned Subdivision and its Sections, together with any additional projects, Subdivisions or subjects expressly assigned through a separate effective assignment.
- `EE` — Executive Engineer: all projects, Subdivisions, Sections and project-related records under the officer’s assigned Division, together with any additional-charge Division or approved jurisdiction expressly assigned.
- `SE` — Superintending Engineer: all Divisions, Subdivisions, Sections and projects under the officer’s assigned Circle, together with any additional Circle or approved jurisdiction expressly assigned.
- `CE` — Chief Engineer: all Circles, Divisions, Subdivisions, Sections and projects under the officer’s assigned Regional Office. A Chief Engineer shall not receive access outside that Regional Office unless an additional jurisdiction is expressly assigned.

The scope of a Head of Office shall ordinarily include visibility over the office and all descendant offices within that officer’s approved jurisdiction. However, visibility shall not automatically grant every operational power. Authority to create, edit, issue instructions, forward, verify, approve, audit, delete or administer records shall depend on the officer’s active functional post and specific approved permissions.

Junior Engineer, Sectional Engineer, Assistant Engineer Grade-II and other project-level officers shall not receive every project of their office merely because they are posted there. Their access shall arise from explicit project, Section, chainage, subject or jurisdiction assignments.

Where one officer holds multiple substantive, temporary, deputation or additional-charge assignments, each assignment shall be recorded separately with:

- office;
- designation;
- functional post;
- jurisdiction;
- effective start date;
- effective end date, where applicable;
- reporting officer;
- application role; and
- specific permissions.

The system shall combine only the officer’s currently active assignments. Expired, deactivated or future assignments shall not contribute to access.

Functional appointments shall use assignment-based scope as follows:

- A Project Officer in a Division Office shall access only the Subdivision, projects or subjects assigned to that Project Officer.
- A Project Officer in a Circle Office shall access only the Division, projects or subjects assigned to that Project Officer.
- A Project Officer in a Regional Office shall access only the Division or group of Divisions, projects or subjects specifically assigned.
- A Personal Assistant in a Division or Circle Office shall access only the matters and jurisdiction specifically delegated by the concerned Head of Office.
- A Personal Assistant in a Regional Office shall access only the Circle or group of Circles and subjects specifically assigned.
- Auditors and accounts personnel shall access only the office, Subdivision, Division, Circle, project, contract, bill, tender, account or subject assigned to them.
- The Divisional Accountant may supervise the audit and accounts work of the Division but shall not receive general engineering or project-administration powers unless separately authorised.

Inherited superior-office visibility shall follow the approved hierarchy:

`Regional Office → Circle Office → Division Office → Subdivision Office → Section`

Lower offices shall not automatically receive access to parent-office records or to sibling offices. Cross-office or cross-jurisdiction access shall require a separate explicit, effective and auditable assignment.

The same effective-scope rules shall be enforced consistently in database RLS policies, direct record URLs, data mutations, attachments, search, notifications, dashboards, reports, exports and all user-interface controls. The interface shall not display records or commands that the user is not authorised to access or perform.

The `platform_admin` context shall remain separate from ordinary engineering hierarchy. Platform-wide administrative authority shall not arise from the designation of Chief Engineer, Superintending Engineer, Executive Engineer or any other departmental post. It shall require a separately approved Platform Administrator account or context, with all activity recorded in the audit trail.

### 6. Decide how office jurisdiction and explicit project assignment combine

**Question requiring confirmation:** Should project visibility require office jurisdiction, explicit assignment, both, or either one?

**Why this matters:** The audit target describes office scope plus explicit project access, while current project visibility is broad and `project_user_access` is used mainly for action recipients. The combination rule determines every user's effective dataset.

**Available options:**

1. Either approved office jurisdiction or an active explicit project grant (union).
2. Both office jurisdiction and explicit project assignment (intersection).
3. Office scope only, with explicit grants used only for exceptions.
4. Explicit assignment only.

**Codex recommendation:** Use the union of approved office-derived scope and active, time-bounded explicit project grants. Require every explicit exception to record its type, approver, reason, dates, and action-receipt permission. A user with neither source receives no project access.

**Affected areas:** `projects`, `offices`, `user_assignments`, `project_user_access`, effective-scope helper, project RLS, all dependent modules, direct URLs, exports, and migration comparison tests.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt a controlled union model. Project visibility shall arise from either:

1. an active office or functional assignment that gives the user approved jurisdiction over that project; or
2. an active, explicit project-jurisdiction assignment specifically granted to that user.

A user who satisfies neither condition shall have no access to the project or to any dependent correspondence, actions, inspections, calendar entries, reports, notifications, search results, attachments or direct project URLs.

Office-derived access shall not mean that every employee posted in an office automatically receives all projects of that office. It shall arise only from the user’s active office assignment, functional post, designated jurisdiction and approved permissions. For example:

- a Head of Office may receive the authorised descendant scope of that office;
- a Project Officer shall receive only the Subdivisions, Divisions, projects or subjects specifically assigned to that Project Officer;
- a Personal Assistant shall receive only the Circles, Divisions, Subdivisions, projects or matters specifically assigned by the concerned Head of Office;
- an Auditor or accounts officer shall receive only the office, project, contract, bill, tender, account or subject assigned for scrutiny;
- Junior Engineer, Sectional Engineer and Assistant Engineer Grade-II shall normally receive only explicitly assigned projects, project portions, chainages, Sections or subjects.

An explicit project grant may supplement normal office jurisdiction for temporary duty, additional charge, deputation, cross-office assistance, special audit, verification, legal work, emergency work or another approved purpose. Every such grant shall separately record:

- the user;
- the project or defined project portion;
- the type of grant;
- the granting authority;
- the reason;
- the effective start date;
- the effective end date, where applicable;
- whether the user may merely view or may also receive actions;
- the permitted operations, such as view, create, edit, instruct, process, forward, verify, approve, audit, upload or administer;
- the related office, assignment or order reference; and
- revocation or expiry status.

Explicit grants shall be time-bounded wherever the assignment is temporary. Expired, revoked, future-dated, inactive or unapproved grants shall not contribute to access.

Office-derived scope and explicit project grants shall be combined as a union only for visibility. Operational authority shall still depend on the user’s active functional post and specific permissions. Therefore, the ability to view a project shall not automatically permit the user to issue instructions, edit records, forward matters, verify progress, approve work, audit bills, delete records or administer the project.

Lower offices shall not obtain access to parent-office or sibling-office projects merely because they belong to the same hierarchy. Cross-office or cross-jurisdiction access shall require a separate explicit and auditable grant.

The same effective-scope rule shall be enforced consistently in database RLS policies, direct record URLs, data mutations, search, notifications, dashboards, reports, exports, attachments and every user-interface module. Records outside the user’s effective scope shall not be displayed and shall not be retrievable by manually entering a URL or identifier.

Platform Administrator access shall remain separate from ordinary office-derived and explicit project scope. Administrative access shall not be inferred from engineering designation, seniority or office posting and shall require the separately approved Platform Administrator account or context.

### 7. Define the authoritative officer context and multiple-assignment rules

**Available options:**

**Codex recommendation:** Make time-bounded `user_assignments` authoritative. Default to the active substantive/primary assignment, allow the user to select an active additional or temporary charge, and compute scope from only the selected Officer context plus separately approved explicit project grants. Keep profile office fields as display/cache data, not authorization data.

**Affected areas:** `profiles.office_id`, `profiles.office_name`, `user_assignments`, context state, session behavior, header office label, scope helpers, access audit, assignment backfill, and revocation tests.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Make `user_assignments` the authoritative source for office, post, jurisdiction and access context. The fields `profiles.office_id` and `profiles.office_name` shall be retained only as display, fallback or cached information and shall not independently grant authorization.

Every assignment shall be recorded as a separate row with its own:

- user;
- office;
- official designation;
- functional post;
- assignment type;
- jurisdiction;
- application role;
- specific permissions;
- effective start date;
- effective end date, where applicable;
- reporting officer or Head of Office;
- order or authority reference;
- approval status;
- active, expired, revoked or suspended status; and
- substantive, temporary, deputation or additional-charge nature.

The recognised assignment types shall include at least:

- substantive or primary assignment;
- additional charge;
- temporary assignment;
- deputation;
- special duty;
- project-specific assignment; and
- audit, verification, legal or other approved functional assignment.

The user’s substantive or primary assignment shall be the default Officer context after login. Where the user has another active additional-charge, temporary, deputation or special-duty assignment, the user shall be permitted to switch deliberately to that context through a visible context selector.

Only one Officer context shall be active at a time for ordinary operational work. The effective office label, jurisdiction, available projects, commands, reporting relationships and permissions shown in the application shall be derived from the selected active assignment.

The system shall not automatically union all active assignments into one unrestricted context. This is necessary to prevent accidental cross-office visibility and to preserve a clear audit trail. However, separately approved explicit project grants may remain available in addition to the selected Officer context, subject to their own scope, dates and permissions.

Changing the Officer context shall not require a second login where the assignments belong to the same ordinary user account. The application shall clearly display the currently selected office, designation or functional post and assignment type in the header or profile area.

Each action performed by the user shall record the assignment context used at that time, including:

- assignment identifier;
- office;
- functional post;
- jurisdiction;
- application role;
- effective permissions; and
- date and time of the action.

Substantive, additional-charge and temporary assignments shall not override one another merely by rank or creation date. Their availability shall depend on approval status and effective dates. The selected context determines operational scope.

The following precedence rules shall apply:

1. A valid and active assignment selected by the user shall control the current Officer context.
2. If no context has been selected in the current session, the active substantive or primary assignment shall be selected by default.
3. If the substantive assignment is inactive, expired, suspended or unavailable, the user shall be asked to select from the remaining active approved assignments.
4. Future-dated assignments shall not be selectable before their start date.
5. Expired, revoked, suspended, inactive or unapproved assignments shall not contribute to access and shall not remain selectable.
6. Where two assignments overlap, both may remain separately selectable, but their scopes and permissions shall not be merged automatically.
7. A temporary or additional-charge assignment shall cease to grant access immediately upon expiry or revocation, except for read-only access to the user’s own historical audit records where permitted.
8. A user shall not continue to receive new actions, notifications or assignments under an expired context.

For additional charge, temporary duty or deputation, an end date shall normally be mandatory. Where an open-ended assignment is legally or administratively necessary, the assignment shall require a recorded reason, approving authority and periodic review.

The Head of Office or authorised administrator shall be able to activate, suspend, extend or terminate an assignment within their approved jurisdiction. Platform-wide assignment creation or correction shall remain restricted to the Platform Administrator context.

The system shall preserve historical assignments and shall not delete them merely because they have expired. Historical records must continue to show the exact office, post and jurisdiction under which each action was originally performed.

Rahul Potdar’s ordinary Sectional Engineer assignment and his Platform Administrator access shall remain separate contexts. Platform Administrator authority shall not be included in the normal Officer-context selector unless the application provides a clearly separate administrative login or explicitly separated administrative workspace. Administrative activity shall be recorded independently in the audit trail.

Any disagreement between `profiles.office_id` and active `user_assignments` shall be treated as a data-quality issue. Authorization shall continue to follow the active assignment records, and the profile display fields shall be corrected through an audited synchronization process rather than being used to override access.

### 8. Define cross-office access, additional charge, and reorganisation behavior

**Question requiring confirmation:** When may a user receive cross-office or cross-circle access, how is additional charge approved, and what happens to access when an office parent or project implementing office changes?

**Why this matters:** Cross-hierarchy exceptions and reorganisations can create silent privilege expansion if existing grants are inherited automatically.

**Available options:**

1. Prohibit cross-circle access; handle any exception as a time-bounded explicit grant.
2. Permit approved additional-charge assignments across the hierarchy.
3. Automatically carry all previous access through reorganisation.

**Codex recommendation:** Prohibit implicit cross-circle access. Use a time-bounded, approved additional-charge assignment or explicit project grant with reason and audit trail. Recompute scope after reorganisation, produce a before/after access report, and require review rather than automatically preserving every inherited grant.

**Affected areas:** `offices.parent_office_id`, `projects.implementing_office_id`, `user_assignments`, `project_user_access`, audit records, ancestry helper, migration/reorganisation scripts, and cross-scope policy tests.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt a controlled exception model. No user shall receive cross-office, cross-Division, cross-Circle or cross-Regional Office access merely because of designation, seniority, office hierarchy, temporary assistance, previous posting or visibility in another module.

Cross-office access may be granted only through one of the following approved mechanisms:

1. an active additional-charge assignment;
2. an active temporary or deputation assignment;
3. a time-bounded explicit project-jurisdiction grant;
4. a special audit, verification, legal, emergency or technical-assistance assignment; or
5. another specifically approved functional assignment recorded in the system.

Every cross-office assignment or grant shall separately record:

- the user;
- home or substantive office;
- additional or receiving office;
- assignment or grant type;
- office, project, project portion, contract or subject covered;
- functional post and permitted duties;
- approving authority;
- order number or authority reference;
- reason and purpose;
- effective start date;
- effective end date;
- whether action receipt is permitted;
- whether the user may view, create, edit, instruct, process, forward, verify, approve, audit, upload or administer;
- review date, where applicable;
- revocation or expiry status; and
- complete audit history.

Cross-circle and cross-Regional Office access shall not be implicit. It shall require a separately approved and auditable assignment or grant. The approval authority must itself have jurisdiction over the concerned offices or projects, or must act under an order of the competent higher authority.

An additional-charge assignment shall be treated as a separate Officer context and shall not be merged automatically with the user’s substantive assignment. The user shall deliberately select the additional-charge context before exercising authority under it. The application shall clearly display that the user is working under additional charge.

The normal approval chain for additional charge shall follow the competent departmental authority applicable to the post and office concerned. The system shall record the issuing authority and order reference rather than determine legal competence only from designation. A Platform Administrator may enter or activate the assignment in the system but shall not be treated as the departmental approving authority unless separately authorised.

Additional charge, temporary duty, deputation and special assignments shall normally be time-bounded. Where no end date is stated in the official order, the system shall require:

- the reason for the open-ended assignment;
- the issuing authority;
- a periodic review date; and
- confirmation that the assignment remains valid.

Expired, withdrawn, revoked, suspended, future-dated or unapproved cross-office assignments shall not provide access.

Where an office is reorganised, renamed, merged, divided, transferred to another parent office or otherwise changes its position in the hierarchy, the system shall not automatically preserve all previous inherited access.

Before any office-hierarchy reorganisation becomes effective, the system shall produce a review report showing at least:

- the affected office;
- old parent office;
- proposed new parent office;
- affected child offices;
- affected projects;
- affected users and active assignments;
- affected explicit project grants;
- existing and proposed reporting relationships;
- permissions gained;
- permissions lost;
- cross-office or cross-circle access newly created;
- records and attachments affected; and
- grants requiring manual confirmation.

The reorganisation shall require approval by the authorised Platform Administrator and the competent departmental authority, where applicable. Its effective date and order reference shall be recorded.

On the effective date of an approved office reorganisation:

1. office-derived scope shall be recalculated using the new hierarchy;
2. direct and dependent project visibility shall be recomputed;
3. assignments specifically tied to the old office relationship shall be flagged for review;
4. explicit grants shall remain only where their recorded office, project and validity still apply;
5. no new cross-office or cross-circle privilege shall be created silently;
6. doubtful or ambiguous access shall be suspended pending review;
7. affected users shall be notified where their available context or jurisdiction changes; and
8. a before-and-after access report shall be retained in the audit trail.

Where a project’s `implementing_office_id` changes, access shall be recalculated from the new implementing office and approved project-jurisdiction assignments. Users shall not retain project access merely because they had inherited access through the former implementing office.

Before changing a project’s implementing office, the system shall show:

- current implementing office;
- proposed implementing office;
- controlling Division, Circle and Regional Office before and after the change;
- officers and functional posts currently assigned;
- users who will gain or lose access;
- pending actions, inspections, correspondence and calendar items;
- responsible issuer, processor, verifier and competent authority;
- active explicit project grants; and
- unresolved records requiring reassignment.

The change shall require an effective date, reason, authority reference and approval. Pending actions and responsibilities shall not be reassigned automatically without identifying the new responsible officer or office.

Historical records shall preserve the office, assignment and project context that existed when each record or action was created. Reorganisation shall not rewrite historical audit entries to show the new hierarchy.

Explicit project grants shall not automatically transfer to a successor project, new office, split project or merged project unless the approving authority confirms the transfer. Each retained grant shall be reviewed for its continuing purpose, scope, dates and permissions.

Where an office or project is split, the Platform Administrator shall prepare a controlled mapping of:

- predecessor office or project;
- successor offices or projects;
- projects and records transferred;
- user assignments transferred;
- explicit grants retained, changed or revoked;
- pending actions and responsibilities; and
- effective dates.

Where offices or projects are merged, access shall not become the unrestricted union of all predecessor permissions. Effective access shall be recalculated and unnecessary duplicate or broader grants shall be removed.

Emergency cross-office access may be granted for urgent public safety, disaster response, court compliance or another recorded emergency purpose. Such access shall:

- be limited to the minimum necessary scope;
- have a short expiry period;
- identify the emergency reason and approving authority;
- be prominently marked as emergency access;
- be fully audited; and
- require subsequent review.

No lower office shall receive access to a parent office, sibling office or unrelated project merely because of reorganisation. No higher-office functional officer, including a Personal Assistant, Project Officer or Auditor, shall receive unrestricted descendant access unless that scope is specifically included in the person’s active assignment.

Platform Administrator authority shall remain separate from departmental cross-office jurisdiction. Administrative ability to configure an office, assignment or project shall not itself provide operational visibility or authority over the project records of that office.

All cross-office, additional-charge and reorganisation rules shall be enforced consistently in database RLS policies, direct record URLs, search, notifications, dashboards, reports, exports, attachments, project records, correspondence, actions, inspections, calendar entries and Administration screens.

## Context and administrative-governance decisions

### 9. Define how one person switches between Officer and Platform Administrator contexts

**Question requiring confirmation:** How does a dual-entitled user select a context, which context is the default, how long does it last, is re-authentication required, and what must be audited?

**Why this matters:** Current `profiles.role='admin'` combines administration and operational permissions. A clear active context prevents accidental platform-wide behavior during normal officer work.

**Available options:**

1. Default to Officer context and require an explicit, audited switch to Platform Administrator, with optional re-authentication for sensitive changes.
2. Ask for context at every login.
3. Allow concurrent contexts in one session.

**Codex recommendation:** Default to Officer context. Require an explicit context switch, show the active context persistently, audit entry/exit, use a short administrator-context lifetime, and require recent authentication for high-risk access changes. Do not combine both contexts concurrently.

**Affected areas:** Auth/session handling, `profiles`, future administrator entitlement/context tables or claims, app header/sidebar, Administration route, audit log, RLS helpers, and session refresh/revocation tests.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt a strictly separated dual-context model. A user who is entitled to both ordinary departmental access and Platform Administrator access shall not use both authorities concurrently in the same active context.

The default context after login shall always be the user’s ordinary Officer context. The Officer context shall be derived from the user’s selected active `user_assignments` record and shall show only the office, projects, records, commands and permissions available under that assignment.

Platform Administrator access shall be entered only through a deliberate and clearly labelled context switch. The application shall not enter Platform Administrator context automatically merely because the user has the entitlement.

For Rahul Potdar, the ordinary Sectional Engineer context and the Platform Administrator context shall remain logically separate. Normal engineering work shall be performed only in the Officer context. Platform-wide configuration work shall be performed only after switching to the Platform Administrator context.

The interface shall continuously and prominently display the active context. At minimum, the header or account area shall show:

- active context name;
- active office or “Platform Administration” label;
- official designation or administrator entitlement;
- assignment or context identifier;
- context start time; and
- a visible control to exit or switch context.

The visual appearance of Platform Administrator context shall be clearly distinguishable from the Officer context so that the user cannot reasonably mistake one for the other. A persistent banner, badge or differently labelled workspace shall identify that administrative authority is active.

The context-switch flow shall operate as follows:

1. The user signs in and enters the Officer context by default.
2. The user selects “Switch to Platform Administrator” from the account or context menu.
3. The system verifies that the user has an active, approved Platform Administrator entitlement.
4. The system displays a confirmation screen explaining that the user is entering platform-wide administrative mode.
5. Where required by the sensitivity of the intended operation, the system requests recent re-authentication.
6. The system creates a separate, time-limited Platform Administrator context.
7. The user performs only administrative operations available in that context.
8. On exit, timeout, logout or revocation, the system returns the user to the ordinary Officer context or requires a fresh login.

The two contexts shall not be active simultaneously. Data, commands and permissions from the Officer context shall not be automatically combined with Platform Administrator authority.

Platform Administrator context shall not provide ordinary operational authority over project records merely because the administrator can configure the project, office, user or assignment. Operational viewing, instruction, processing, verification, audit or approval shall continue to require a valid Officer assignment or separately approved operational grant.

The Platform Administrator context may include approved authority to:

- create, edit, activate, deactivate or reorganise offices;
- manage the office hierarchy;
- create and maintain designation and functional-post catalogues;
- approve or correct user identities and assignments;
- create, activate, suspend, expire or revoke assignments;
- manage application roles and permissions;
- create, edit, deactivate or transfer projects;
- manage project jurisdictions and explicit project grants;
- manage authorised administrative configuration;
- review access reports and audit logs; and
- perform other specifically approved platform-governance functions.

The Platform Administrator context shall not automatically permit the user to:

- issue engineering instructions;
- process or verify project work;
- certify progress;
- approve technical or financial matters;
- act as competent authority;
- perform departmental audit;
- receive actions intended for an operational officer; or
- alter operational records outside an expressly authorised administrative correction process.

Every entry into and exit from Platform Administrator context shall be audited. The audit record shall contain at least:

- user identifier;
- ordinary Officer context held before the switch;
- Platform Administrator entitlement identifier;
- date and time of entry;
- authentication or re-authentication method;
- device or session identifier;
- reason for entering the context, where required;
- date and time of exit;
- exit reason, including manual exit, timeout, logout, revocation or session termination; and
- all administrative actions performed during that context.

Every administrative mutation shall record:

- acting user;
- active Platform Administrator context identifier;
- affected office, user, assignment, project, role, permission or configuration;
- previous value;
- new value;
- reason or note;
- authority reference, where applicable;
- date and time; and
- success, failure or rollback status.

Platform Administrator context shall have a limited lifetime. The default maximum continuous duration shall be 30 minutes of administrative activity, subject to later configuration by approved policy.

The context shall expire earlier where:

- the user logs out;
- the user manually exits administrator mode;
- the administrator entitlement is revoked or suspended;
- the session becomes invalid;
- the account is deactivated;
- a security event requires termination; or
- the permitted inactivity period is exceeded.

The inactivity timeout for Platform Administrator context shall be shorter than the ordinary Officer session. After expiry, the user shall return to Officer context and shall need to switch again before performing further administrative work.

Recent re-authentication shall be mandatory before high-risk operations, including:

- granting or removing Platform Administrator entitlement;
- changing administrator roles or permissions;
- changing office parent relationships;
- creating, splitting, merging or deleting offices;
- transferring a project between implementing offices;
- creating broad cross-office or cross-circle access;
- changing RLS-related access configuration;
- bulk assignment or permission changes;
- deleting or permanently deactivating users;
- revoking another administrator;
- changing authentication or security settings;
- exporting sensitive platform-wide access data; and
- any other operation classified as high risk.

Recent authentication shall normally mean successful authentication within the preceding 10 minutes. The system may require immediate re-authentication for especially sensitive actions.

A Platform Administrator shall not be permitted to approve their own new entitlement, expand their own administrative scope without independent approval, or remove the last remaining active Platform Administrator without an approved recovery procedure.

Where a Platform Administrator changes their own ordinary Officer assignment, project jurisdiction or operational permission, the action shall require the same approval and audit safeguards that would apply to another user. Administrative context shall not bypass departmental approval requirements.

A user may have more than one ordinary Officer assignment, but the Officer-context selector and the Platform Administrator-context selector shall remain separate. Switching between ordinary Officer assignments is not the same as entering Platform Administrator context.

The system shall preserve the context used for every historical action. Subsequent changes to the user’s office, designation, assignment or administrator entitlement shall not rewrite earlier audit records.

The Administration route and administrative commands shall be inaccessible in Officer context unless the user also holds a separately approved office-level administrative permission for limited work within that office. Such limited `officer_admin` authority shall remain distinct from platform-wide `platform_admin` authority.

The system shall prevent direct URLs, browser history, cached pages, session restoration or multiple tabs from silently restoring Platform Administrator authority after expiry. Every administrative request shall verify that the Platform Administrator context is still active, valid and unexpired.

If the same account is open in multiple browser tabs, a context change, timeout, revocation or logout shall be reflected across all tabs. No tab shall retain obsolete administrative authority.

The ordinary Officer context shall remain the safe fallback. When there is any uncertainty about the active context, expired entitlement, missing assignment or failed context verification, the system shall deny administrative action and return the user to Officer context.

These rules shall be enforced consistently in authentication, session handling, RLS helpers, application routes, menus, direct URLs, mutations, exports, Administration screens, audit logs and revocation tests.

### 10. Decide whether Platform Administrators can see operational project data in administrator context

**Question requiring confirmation:** While Platform Administrator context is active, may the user view Dashboard, Projects, Correspondence, Actions, Inspections, Calendar, Reports, Search, Notifications, or attachments beyond the user's Officer scope?

### 11. Assign authority over users, offices, posts, projects, and jurisdiction records

**Question requiring confirmation:** Who may create, edit, approve, deactivate, reactivate, or delete users, offices, posts/designations, projects, office assignments, and project-jurisdiction grants?

**Why this matters:** These records define authorization. Broad editor permissions or hard deletion can change access without a durable review trail.

**Available options:**

1. Platform Administrators exclusively manage all access-defining master data.
2. Delegate selected project or office operations to scoped senior officers.
3. Retain the current coarse `admin`/`editor` mutation model.

**Codex recommendation:** Initially restrict user approval/deactivation, offices, posts, officer assignments, and jurisdiction grants to Platform Administrator context. Restrict project creation and scope-changing edits to Platform Administrators until a separate delegated permission is designed. Prefer deactivate/retire over hard delete; reserve deletion for exceptional correction with audit and dependency checks.

**Affected areas:** `profiles`, `offices`, future post catalogue, `user_assignments`, `projects`, `project_user_access`, Administration UI, project forms, RLS INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE policies, audit retention, and migration governance.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt a controlled and auditable authority model. Access-defining master data shall not be editable merely because a user holds a senior engineering designation or the ordinary application role of editor.

Platform Administrator context shall initially be the principal authority for creating, approving, modifying, activating, deactivating, retiring and correcting access-defining records.

The following records shall be treated as access-defining master data:

- user identity and approval records;
- office records and office hierarchy;
- official designation catalogue;
- functional-post catalogue;
- application roles and permission definitions;
- officer assignments;
- substantive, temporary, deputation and additional-charge records;

## Official designations and functional posts

Only Platform Administrator context shall create, modify, activate, deactivate or retire official designation and functional-post catalogue entries.

Canonical codes shall be immutable after use. Where a code has been created incorrectly, it shall normally be retired and replaced by a new approved entry rather than silently rewritten.

Changes to display labels, category, rank, equivalence or scope metadata shall be audited.

No user shall directly enter an unrestricted free-text designation or functional post for authorization purposes.

The Platform Administrator may map legacy free-text values to approved catalogue entries, but the original legacy value shall be preserved for audit and migration review.

## Officer assignments

Only Platform Administrator context shall create, activate, suspend, expire, revoke or correct access-bearing officer assignments in the system.

The Head of Office or other competent authority may initiate or recommend an assignment. The system action shall be based on an approved order, posting instruction, additional-charge order, deputation order, temporary-duty order or other authorised reference.

Every assignment shall record:

- user;
- official designation;
- functional post;
- office;
- jurisdiction;
- assignment type;
- substantive, temporary, additional-charge, deputation or special-duty nature;
- effective start date;
- effective end date, where applicable;
- reporting officer;
- application role;
- specific permissions;
- order or authority reference;
- approving authority;
- reason or remarks;
- status; and
- audit history.

Assignment approval shall not be inferred solely from a user’s designation, seniority or existing office.

No assignment shall be hard deleted after it has been used for operational activity. It shall be expired, revoked, suspended or corrected while preserving its history.

## Projects

Only Platform Administrator context shall initially create a new project master record.

Project creation shall require:

- project code;
- approved project name;
- highway or scheme reference;
- package or section reference;
- project type;
- mode, where applicable;
- primary implementing office;
- controlling office derived through hierarchy;
- effective start date;
- project status;
- authority or sanction reference;
- responsible office recommendation; and
- audit metadata.

The concerned Executive Engineer, Superintending Engineer, Chief Engineer or other authorised Head of Office may propose or recommend creation or modification of a project falling within their jurisdiction. The Platform Administrator shall perform the system configuration after verifying the authority and required fields.

Ordinary operational officers may update only the project fields expressly delegated to them, such as approved progress, operational status or other authorised project-management information. They shall not alter access-defining fields merely because they can edit the project.

The following project changes shall remain restricted to Platform Administrator context unless a separate delegated permission is approved:

- changing project identifier;
- changing project implementing office;
- changing controlling office;
- transferring the project to another Division, Circle or Regional Office;
- splitting or merging projects;
- changing project jurisdiction;
- activating, deactivating or retiring the project;
- changing project visibility;
- changing assigned officer scope;
- deleting a mistakenly created project; and
- making bulk project-access changes.

Changing the implementing office shall trigger access recalculation and review before becoming effective.

## Project-jurisdiction and explicit project grants

Only Platform Administrator context shall create, activate, modify, expire, revoke or correct project-jurisdiction assignments and explicit project-access grants.

The concerned Head of Office may recommend a grant for an officer, Project Officer, Personal Assistant, Auditor or other authorised person. The Platform Administrator shall verify and record the grant.

Each project-jurisdiction or explicit grant shall include:

- user or office receiving access;
- project or defined project portion;
- geographical, chainage, package, subject or module scope;
- reason;
- grant type;
- granting authority;
- approving authority;
- order or reference;
- effective start date;
- effective end date, where applicable;
- whether action receipt is allowed;
- permitted operations;
- review date;
- revocation or expiry status; and
- audit history.

Broad, indefinite or platform-wide project grants shall not be used as a substitute for properly modelled office assignments.

## Delegated office-level administration

A separate limited `officer_admin` permission may later be introduced for selected senior officers or authorised office staff.

Such delegated authority may include limited operations within the officer’s approved office and jurisdiction, such as:

- recommending user approval;
- maintaining non-authoritative contact information;
- proposing assignments;
- proposing project creation;
- recommending project-jurisdiction changes;
- activating or deactivating local directory entries;
- correcting non-access-bearing display information; and
- reviewing access reports for that office.

Delegated office-level authority shall not permit the user to:

- create or remove Platform Administrators;
- change office hierarchy;
- create canonical designation codes;
- change RLS or platform permissions;
- grant themselves access;
- create unrestricted cross-office access;
- alter another office’s records;
- hard delete users or projects;
- bypass approval requirements; or
- modify access-defining fields outside their assigned jurisdiction.

Every delegated operation shall still be audited and may require Platform Administrator approval before taking effect.

## Deactivation, retirement and deletion

Deactivation or retirement shall be preferred over hard deletion.

A deactivated user, office, assignment, project, designation, post or grant shall remain in historical records and audit reports.

Hard deletion shall be permitted only for exceptional correction where:

- the record was created in error;
- it has no valid dependent operational records;
- it has not been relied upon for authorization or historical action;
- dependency checks pass;
- the reason is recorded;
- recent re-authentication is completed;
- the deletion is independently approved where required; and
- the deletion event is permanently audited.

Where dependencies exist, deletion shall be refused. The record shall instead be corrected, deactivated, retired, superseded or merged through a controlled process.

Historical correspondence, actions, inspections, reports, audit entries and attachments shall not be deleted merely because the related user, office, assignment or project becomes inactive.

## Reactivation

Reactivation shall not automatically restore all previous access.

Before reactivation, the system shall review:

- current designation;
- current office;
- current assignment;
- effective dates;
- previous permissions;
- project jurisdictions;
- explicit grants;
- administrator entitlements;
- reason for earlier deactivation; and
- whether the earlier access remains valid.

Expired, revoked or obsolete access shall not be silently restored.

## Approval and segregation of duties

High-risk access changes should, where practicable, follow a maker-checker model.

The person proposing the change and the person approving it should be different for:

- granting Platform Administrator entitlement;
- office reorganisation;
- cross-circle or cross-Regional Office access;
- broad project-jurisdiction grants;
- project transfer;
- bulk user or assignment changes;
- deletion of access-defining records; and
- modification of security or permission configuration.

Where a separate approver is not available, the system shall require a recorded authority reference and flag the action for subsequent review.

No user shall approve their own:

- administrator entitlement;
- cross-office grant;
- broad project access;
- assignment expansion;
- role elevation; or
- deletion of a record affecting their own access.

## Audit requirements

Every creation, approval, rejection, modification, activation, deactivation, reactivation, retirement, revocation, correction and deletion shall record:

- acting user;
- active context;
- affected record type;
- affected record identifier;
- previous value;
- new value;
- reason;
- authority or order reference;
- approving authority;
- effective date;
- date and time of action;
- success, failure or rollback status; and
- linked review record, where applicable.

Bulk actions shall record both the overall operation and each affected record.

The system shall provide reports identifying:

- active users without assignments;
- assignments without valid offices;
- users with excessive permissions;
- cross-office and cross-circle grants;
- expiring assignments;
- inactive users with active grants;
- projects without implementing offices;
- orphaned jurisdiction records;
- users holding both Officer and Platform Administrator entitlement;
- self-approved changes;
- records pending review; and
- recent high-risk administrative changes.

## Enforcement

These authority rules shall be enforced consistently in:

- Administration screens;
- project forms;
- user and assignment forms;
- office and hierarchy forms;
- designation and functional-post catalogues;
- direct URLs;
- backend APIs;
- database RLS policies;
- database functions and RPCs;
- imports and bulk operations;
- search and reports;
- exports;
- audit logs; and
- migration tools.

The ordinary roles of viewer, editor or departmental senior officer shall not by themselves grant authority to modify access-defining master data.

Until a separately reviewed delegation model is implemented, Platform Administrator context shall remain the exclusive system authority for final changes to users, offices, designations, functional posts, officer assignments, project master scope and project-jurisdiction records.

## Action-workflow decisions

### 12. Define who may issue actions to whom

**Question requiring confirmation:** Which reporting directions are allowed for action issuance, may project-specific officers assign laterally, and who can approve cross-office, cross-circle, or external assignments?

**Why this matters:** Current assignee selection checks project mapping but not organisational reporting hierarchy, and direct API calls are not protected by a hierarchy rule.

**Available options:**

1. Allow downward assignment within the issuer's office chain and relevant project; require an audited exception for lateral, upward, cross-office, or external assignment.
2. Allow any in-project officer to assign any other in-project officer.
3. Allow unrestricted assignment by role.

**Codex recommendation:** Permit normal downward assignment only within the issuer's approved reporting chain and shared project scope. Permit upward submission as a distinct workflow event. Disallow lateral and cross-office issuance by default; require a named competent authority, reason, expiry, and audit record for exceptions.

**Affected areas:** `action_items`, `action_assignees`, `project_user_access.can_receive_actions`, `user_assignments`, reporting-hierarchy helper, Action creation/editing/forwarding, RLS write policies, and positive/negative direct API tests.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt a controlled reporting-hierarchy and shared-project model for issuing actions. A user shall not be able to issue an action merely because both users can view the same project.

Action authority shall depend on:

- the issuer’s selected active Officer context;
- the issuer’s office and functional assignment;
- the recipient’s active assignment;
- the approved reporting relationship;
- shared project or jurisdiction scope;
- the type of action or workflow event;
- effective assignment dates;
- specific action permissions; and
- any separately approved exception.

## General rule

Normal action issuance shall be permitted downward within the issuer’s approved administrative or functional reporting chain and only for a project, project portion, subject or jurisdiction that is visible to both the issuer and recipient.

A user shall not receive an assignee merely because that user:

- belongs to the same department;
- is posted in the same office;
- has a lower designation;
- can view the project;
- previously worked on the project;
- appears in the office directory; or
- has the general application role of editor or administrator.

The assignee list shall contain only currently active and authorised recipients for that particular action.

## Normal engineering reporting chain

For ordinary engineering work, the normal downward chain shall follow the approved office hierarchy and active assignments:

`Chief Engineer → Superintending Engineer → Executive Engineer → Deputy Engineer / Sub-Divisional Engineer / authorised Subdivision Head → Assistant Engineer Grade-II / Sectional Engineer / Junior Engineer or other assigned project-level officer`

The following normal issue directions may be allowed, subject to shared scope and permission:

- Chief Engineer to Superintending Engineers, Executive Engineers or specifically assigned Regional Office functional officers within the Chief Engineer’s Regional Office;
- Superintending Engineer to Executive Engineers, Circle-level Project Officers, Personal Assistants, Auditors or other officers specifically assigned within that Circle;
- Executive Engineer to Subdivision Heads, Division-level Project Officers, Personal Assistants, Divisional Accountant, Auditors or other officers specifically assigned within that Division;
- Subdivision Head to Junior Engineers, Sectional Engineers, Assistant Engineers Grade-II, Project Officers or other officers specifically assigned to that Subdivision, Section or project;
- an authorised project-level officer to subordinate staff specifically assigned under that officer for the same project, Section, chainage or subject; and
- a Head of Office to functional officers working directly under that Head of Office, limited to their recorded jurisdiction and duties.

A superior officer may issue directly to a lower-level officer within the same authorised hierarchy where administratively necessary. The system should ordinarily show the normal intermediate reporting route and record when the intermediate level is bypassed.

Direct issuance across several levels shall not silently change the recipient’s reporting officer or permanent assignment.

## Shared project and subject requirement

Except for office-administration actions, the issuer and recipient shall normally share at least one approved scope element, such as:

- the same project;
- the same project package;
- the same Section;
- the same Subdivision jurisdiction;
- the same defined chainage;
- the same correspondence matter;
- the same inspection;
- the same contract;
- the same land-acquisition matter;
- the same audit subject; or
- another specifically recorded common subject.

A superior office’s inherited visibility may satisfy the shared-scope requirement only where the issuer’s active functional post authorises action issuance for that descendant office or project.

Visibility alone shall not grant the right to issue, edit, process, verify, approve or close an action.

## Project Officers

A Project Officer may issue actions only where the Project Officer’s active assignment expressly includes action-issuing authority.

A Project Officer in a Division Office may issue actions only for the Subdivision, projects or subjects assigned to that Project Officer and only to officers falling within that approved jurisdiction.

A Project Officer in a Circle Office may issue actions only for the Division, projects or subjects assigned to that Project Officer.

A Project Officer in a Regional Office may issue actions only for the Divisions, Circles, projects or subjects specifically assigned to that Project Officer.

A Project Officer shall not automatically receive authority over every officer or project of the parent office merely because the Project Officer is posted there.

## Personal Assistants

A Personal Assistant may issue, forward, return or coordinate actions only where such authority is expressly included in the active functional assignment issued by the concerned Head of Office.

A Personal Assistant shall normally act on behalf of the concerned Head of Office for the Circles, Divisions, Subdivisions, projects or subjects specifically allocated to that Personal Assistant.

The system shall clearly record whether the action was:

- independently issued by the Personal Assistant under delegated authority;
- issued on behalf of the Head of Office;
- forwarded for the Head of Office;
- returned for compliance;
- marked for examination; or
- merely coordinated without issuing authority.

A Personal Assistant shall not inherit all powers of the Head of Office merely from the functional title.

## Auditors and accounts personnel

Auditors, the Divisional Accountant and other accounts personnel may issue audit observations, scrutiny requirements, document requests, return memos and financial-compliance actions only for the office, project, contract, bill, tender, estimate, account or subject specifically assigned to them.

Such personnel may issue actions to the concerned project or office officers where required for audit or scrutiny, even though the direction is not part of the normal engineering command chain.

This shall be treated as a recognised functional reporting relationship, not unrestricted lateral authority.

Auditors and accounts personnel shall not issue general engineering instructions, approve technical work, modify project scope or exercise administrative control unless separately authorised.

## Upward submission

A subordinate officer shall not use ordinary downward “Issue Action” authority to command a superior officer.

The system shall provide separate upward workflow events, including:

- submit for review;
- submit for approval;
- submit for verification;
- seek decision;
- request clarification;
- report compliance;
- report inability or delay;
- escalate overdue matter;
- refer technical issue;
- forward recommendation; and
- return completed work.

An upward submission shall identify:

- the originating officer;
- the reporting or approving officer;
- the action or record concerned;
- the purpose of submission;
- supporting remarks;
- attachments, where applicable;
- date and time;
- expected decision, where applicable; and
- subsequent return, approval or instruction.

An upward submission shall not be represented as an instruction issued by the subordinate to the superior.

## Lateral coordination

Ordinary lateral action issuance between officers of equivalent level shall not be permitted merely because both officers are mapped to the same project.

Where coordination between equivalent officers is required, the system shall use one of the following:

- request for information;
- coordination request;
- consultation;
- copy for information;
- joint task created by a common superior;
- transfer through the reporting officer;
- approved functional assignment; or
- separately approved lateral-action permission.

A lateral coordination request shall not give one officer supervisory authority over the other.

For a joint task, the common competent officer shall remain the issuer or controlling officer, while multiple officers may be recorded as responsible participants.

## Cross-Subdivision, cross-Division, cross-Circle and cross-Regional Office actions

Cross-office action issuance shall not be allowed by default.

It may be permitted only through:

- an active additional-charge assignment;
- a deputation or temporary-duty assignment;
- an approved cross-office functional assignment;
- an explicit project-jurisdiction grant containing action authority;
- a competent-authority order;
- an authorised audit, verification, legal or technical-assistance assignment;
- an emergency public-safety or court-compliance assignment; or
- another specifically approved exception.

The approving authority shall have jurisdiction over both the issuing and receiving offices or shall act under an order of the competent higher authority.

Every cross-office exception shall record:

- issuer;
- recipient;
- originating office;
- receiving office;
- project, project portion, contract or subject;
- reason;
- type of exception;
- permitted action operations;
- approving authority;
- order or reference;
- effective start date;
- effective end date;
- review date, where applicable;
- whether further forwarding is permitted;
- completion or revocation status; and
- complete audit history.

Cross-Circle and cross-Regional Office action authority shall always require a separately approved and auditable arrangement. It shall never arise automatically through project visibility.

## External recipients

External persons or organisations shall not be treated as ordinary internal action assignees.

Where work must be communicated to a contractor, consultant, utility agency, CALA, Railway authority, police authority, local body or another external entity, the system shall distinguish between:

- internal action;
- official correspondence;
- external compliance request;
- external submission request;
- meeting or coordination item;
- contract instruction;
- statutory notice; and
- information-only communication.

An external action or request shall require:

- approved external organisation or contact;
- issuing office;
- competent issuing officer;
- project or subject;
- communication type;
- authority or contract provision, where applicable;
- due date;
- attachment or letter reference;
- acknowledgement status;
- response status; and
- audit history.

Only an authorised officer having jurisdiction over the project or subject may issue an external action.

A Project Officer, Personal Assistant, Auditor or other functional officer may prepare or process the communication, but final external issuance shall depend on the authority recorded in that person’s assignment.

## Action creation permissions

To create an action, the issuer shall have an active permission such as:

- `issue_action`;
- `issue_instruction`;
- `assign_task`;
- `request_compliance`;
- `raise_audit_observation`;
- `request_information`;
- `create_joint_task`;
- `forward_action`;
- `return_action`;
- `verify_action`;
- `approve_action`; or
- another approved action permission.

Permissions shall be specific. Permission to view or edit a project shall not automatically include permission to issue actions.

The system shall calculate eligible recipients from the intersection of:

- recipient’s active assignment;
- recipient’s approved project or jurisdiction scope;
- recipient’s permission to receive that action type;
- valid reporting or functional relationship;
- effective dates;
- issuer’s own scope and authority; and
- any approved exception.

## Receiving actions

A user may receive actions only where an active assignment or explicit grant permits action receipt.

The field represented by `project_user_access.can_receive_actions` shall not be sufficient by itself. It shall operate together with:

- active user status;
- approved account status;
- active assignment;
- project or jurisdiction scope;
- reporting relationship;
- permitted action type;
- effective dates; and
- absence of suspension or revocation.

A user who can view a project but is not authorised to receive actions shall not appear in the assignee list.

## Multiple assignees

An action may have:

- one lead assignee;
- one or more supporting assignees;
- a verifying officer;
- an approving or competent authority;
- information recipients; and
- an issuing or controlling officer.

Each role shall be recorded separately. The system shall not treat all named persons as having identical responsibility.

For joint responsibility:

- one person shall normally be identified as the lead assignee;
- each supporting assignee’s scope shall be recorded;
- progress may be submitted separately or jointly;
- verification responsibility shall be distinct;
- completion shall depend on the configured workflow; and
- the action history shall identify which user performed each step.

## Forwarding and reassignment

An assignee may forward or reassign an action only where specifically permitted.

Forwarding may be used for:

- sending the action to a subordinate officer;
- seeking assistance;
- transferring the action to the proper jurisdiction;
- obtaining technical examination;
- obtaining audit review;
- submitting upward;
- returning for clarification; or
- correcting an incorrectly selected recipient.

Every forward or reassignment shall record:

- previous assignee;
- new assignee;
- forwarding user;
- reason;
- whether responsibility was transferred or merely shared;
- remaining due date;
- changed due date, if approved;
- authority for cross-office transfer;
- remarks;
- date and time; and
- audit history.

An assignee shall not forward an action outside the authorised office or project scope unless an approved exception exists.

Where responsibility is transferred, the previous assignee shall remain visible in history but shall no longer hold active responsibility unless retained as a supporting assignee.

## Action approval and verification

The issuer, assignee, verifier and approver shall be separate workflow roles.

The same person should not ordinarily:

- issue an action;
- claim its completion;
- verify the completion; and
- finally approve or close it.

For routine matters, the issuer may be allowed to verify and close the action where departmental practice permits.

For high-risk matters, including financial approval, technical acceptance, safety compliance, audit compliance, access-control changes or statutory compliance, maker-checker separation shall be enforced.

A subordinate’s submitted progress shall not become official merely because it was entered. Only progress verified by an authorised verifying officer shall be treated as official.

## Self-assignment

A user may create a personal task or self-assigned reminder within the user’s own approved scope where the action type permits it.

A self-assigned task shall be clearly labelled and shall not be treated as an official instruction from a superior officer.

A user shall not use self-assignment to:

- bypass a required competent authority;
- approve their own work;
- verify their own high-risk compliance;
- create false reporting history; or
- obtain access to an otherwise unauthorised project.

## Due dates and escalation

The issuer shall set a reasonable due date and, where applicable, a reminder date.

Escalation shall follow the recipient’s active reporting chain and project jurisdiction.

An overdue action may be escalated to:

- the recipient’s reporting officer;
- the issuer;
- the project’s controlling officer;
- the Head of Office;
- another configured reviewing authority; or
- a competent higher authority.

Escalation visibility shall not grant the escalated officer unrestricted access to unrelated records.

## Change in posting or jurisdiction

When an issuer or assignee’s assignment expires, is revoked, changes office or loses project jurisdiction, the system shall review all open actions connected to that assignment.

The system shall identify:

- actions issued by the user;
- actions assigned to the user;
- actions awaiting the user’s verification;
- actions awaiting approval;
- overdue actions;
- cross-office actions;
- supporting assignments; and
- unresolved responsibilities.

Open responsibility shall not be silently retained under an expired assignment.

The competent officer shall reassign, retain through an approved temporary arrangement, close or otherwise dispose of each affected action.

Historical action records shall continue to show the office, assignment, designation and context existing at the time each event occurred.

## Platform Administrator separation

Platform Administrator context shall not provide ordinary authority to issue engineering, financial, audit or administrative actions for operational projects.

A Platform Administrator may:

- configure action types;
- configure workflow rules;
- configure permissions;
- correct invalid action metadata through an audited administrative-correction process;
- manage action-related master data; and
- review access and workflow audit reports.

To issue or process an operational action, the user shall return to an authorised Officer context or receive a separate approved emergency-support assignment.

## Direct API and database enforcement

The hierarchy and recipient rules shall be enforced in the database and backend, not only in the visible assignee dropdown.

Before inserting or changing an action or assignee, the system shall validate:

- authenticated user;
- active Officer context;
- issuer assignment;
- recipient assignment;
- shared project or subject scope;
- reporting or functional relationship;
- action-type permission;
- recipient’s permission to receive actions;
- effective dates;
- cross-office approval, where required;
- external-recipient authority, where applicable; and
- absence of suspension, expiry or revocation.

A direct API request attempting an unauthorised assignment shall be rejected even if the user manually supplies a valid user identifier.

## Audit requirements

Every action issuance, submission, forward, reassignment, return, escalation, verification, approval, rejection, completion, reopening and cancellation shall record:

- action identifier;
- acting user;
- active Officer context;
- acting assignment;
- issuer;
- previous and new assignees;
- project or subject;
- action type;
- reporting or exception basis;
- previous and new workflow stage;
- previous and new due date;
- reason or remarks;
- approving authority, where applicable;
- order or reference, where applicable;
- date and time;
- success, failure or rollback status; and
- affected attachments or linked records.

The system shall provide reports identifying:

- actions issued outside the normal reporting chain;
- lateral actions;
- cross-office and cross-circle actions;
- external actions;
- actions assigned to inactive users;
- actions assigned under expired assignments;
- users receiving actions without valid jurisdiction;
- self-assigned official actions;
- actions lacking a lead assignee;
- actions lacking a verifier where verification is required;
- overdue actions;
- repeatedly reassigned actions; and
- direct API assignment attempts rejected by policy.

## Enforcement consistency

These rules shall be applied consistently in:

- Action creation;
- Action editing;
- assignee selection;
- forwarding;
- reassignment;
- upward submission;
- progress submission;
- verification;
- approval;

- reopening;
- Dashboard;

- Notifications;
- Search;
- Reports;
- exports;
- direct URLs;
- APIs;
- database functions;
- RLS policies;
- attachments; and
- audit logs.

The interface shall not display an action command or recipient that the current user is not authorised to use.

Where no valid reporting relationship or approved exception exists, the action shall not be issued.

### 13. Determine competent-authority and verifier permissions

**Question requiring confirmation:** How are competent authorities and verifiers selected, what scope must they share with the action, may the issuer verify, and must the verifier be different from the submitter?

**Why this matters:** Current dropdowns can use a broad user list, and current verification checks include administrator, verifier, issuer, or creator. This can exceed the intended hierarchy.

**Available options:**

1. Resolve authority and verifier from active post, office hierarchy, project scope, and workflow stage; require separation from the submitter.
2. Allow the issuer to act as default authority/verifier.
3. Permit any approved active user selected by the issuer.

**Codex recommendation:** Require both authority and verifier to have active, effective project and hierarchy entitlement. Require the verifier to differ from the progress submitter. Allow issuer-as-verifier only where an approved workflow rule explicitly permits it and records that choice; do not grant verification through Platform Administrator context.

**Affected areas:** `action_items.competent_authority_id`, `action_items.verifier_id`, `action_progress_updates`, `action_workflow_events`, authorized directory resolver, Actions UI, verification/processing RLS, and workflow-history audit.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt an assignment-based competent-authority and verification model. Competent authority and verifier permissions shall arise from the user’s selected active Officer context, approved functional post, office hierarchy, project or subject jurisdiction, workflow stage and specific permissions. Designation, seniority, project visibility or Platform Administrator entitlement alone shall not provide authority to verify or approve an action.

## Separate workflow roles

The system shall separately record the following roles wherever applicable:

- issuer;
- lead assignee;
- supporting assignee;
- progress submitter;
- processing officer;
- verifier;
- competent authority;
- approving officer;
- closing officer; and
- information recipient.

One person may hold more than one role only where the approved workflow expressly permits it. The system shall not automatically treat the issuer, creator, administrator or senior-most visible user as the verifier or competent authority.

## Selection of competent authority

The competent authority shall be determined from:

- the nature and category of the action;
- project, package, contract, office or subject concerned;
- applicable office hierarchy;
- the officer’s active assignment;
- functional post;
- financial, technical, administrative or statutory competence;
- delegated powers, where applicable;
- effective assignment dates;
- approved jurisdiction;
- action value or risk level, where relevant;
- workflow stage; and
- applicable departmental order, contract provision, circular or authority reference.

The competent authority shall ordinarily be the officer formally empowered to take the required decision, approval or final administrative action.

The system shall not infer legal or departmental competence solely from designation. Where competency depends on delegated powers, monetary limits, technical sanction powers, contract provisions or a specific government order, the relevant authority reference and applicable limit shall be recorded.

## Normal competent-authority hierarchy

Subject to the actual departmental delegation and functional assignment, the system may ordinarily resolve competent authority through the approved hierarchy:

`Chief Engineer → Superintending Engineer → Executive Engineer → Deputy Engineer / Sub-Divisional Engineer / authorised Subdivision Head → assigned project-level officer`

However, the next superior officer shall not automatically be treated as competent authority for every matter.

For example:

- routine field execution may be decided at project or Subdivision level;
- Division-level administrative or contractual matters may require the Executive Engineer;
- Circle-level technical, supervisory or delegated matters may require the Superintending Engineer;
- Regional Office matters may require the Chief Engineer;
- audit scrutiny may require the Divisional Accountant, Auditor or another designated accounts authority;
- financial approval shall follow the applicable financial delegation;
- technical approval shall follow the applicable technical delegation;
- land-acquisition, legal, Railway, forest, utility or statutory matters may require a separately identified competent authority; and
- contract decisions shall follow the applicable contract conditions and delegated authority.

MARGDARSHI shall record the authority relevant to that particular workflow rather than assume that one senior officer is competent for all action types.

## Selection of verifier

A verifier shall be selected only from users who have:

- an approved and active account;
- an active Officer assignment;
- valid project, contract, office or subject jurisdiction;
- the approved functional role or verification permission;
- effective assignment dates;
- access to the underlying action and necessary evidence;
- authority to examine the type of work concerned; and
- no suspension, expiry or revocation affecting that permission.

The verifier shall be technically or functionally capable of verifying the claimed work.

Examples may include:

- an authorised superior engineering officer verifying field or technical progress;
- an assigned inspecting officer verifying site compliance;
- an Auditor or accounts officer verifying financial or scrutiny compliance;
- a designated officer verifying correspondence compliance;
- a project-specific verifying officer;
- a competent technical officer appointed for specialised work; or
- another officer specifically authorised through an approved assignment.

A user shall not appear in the verifier dropdown merely because the user can view the project.

## Shared scope requirement

The verifier and competent authority shall have valid scope over the relevant action through one or more of the following:

- the same project;
- the same project package;
- the same project portion or chainage;
- the same implementing or controlling office hierarchy;
- the same contract;
- the same inspection;
- the same correspondence or compliance matter;
- the same bill, estimate, tender or audit subject;
- the same land-acquisition, legal, Railway, forest or utility matter;
- an approved superior-office jurisdiction;
- a project-specific verification assignment; or
- another explicit and auditable jurisdiction grant.

Superior-office visibility may satisfy the shared-scope requirement only where the officer’s active functional assignment and permissions authorise verification or approval of that descendant matter.

No verifier or competent authority shall be selectable outside the action’s approved scope unless a separately approved cross-office or specialist assignment exists.

## Separation from progress submitter

The verifier shall ordinarily be different from the person who submitted the progress or compliance claim.

A user shall not verify:

- their own claimed physical progress;
- their own financial compliance;
- their own site observation;
- their own supporting evidence;
- their own high-risk statutory compliance;
- their own access-control work;
- their own technical acceptance;
- their own safety certification; or
- any other matter requiring independent checking.

Where multiple officers jointly submit progress, none of the submitting officers shall act as the sole verifier of that same claim.

The system shall enforce separation between submitter and verifier for all workflows classified as requiring independent verification.

## Issuer acting as verifier

The issuer shall not automatically become the verifier.

The issuer may act as verifier only where:

- the workflow type expressly permits issuer verification;
- departmental practice permits the issuer to examine and accept the compliance;
- the issuer has valid project and functional jurisdiction;
- the issuer has the specific `verify_action` permission;
- the issuer is not the progress submitter;
- the matter is not classified as requiring an independent verifier;
- no conflict of interest exists; and
- the choice is recorded in the workflow and audit trail.

Issuer-as-verifier may be appropriate for routine instructions where the issuing officer is responsible for checking compliance.

Issuer-as-verifier shall not ordinarily be permitted for:

- financial approvals;
- bills and payments;
- technical acceptance requiring independent certification;
- safety-critical work;
- statutory compliance;
- audit objections;
- access-control changes;
- administrator entitlement changes;
- actions initiated and substantially executed by the same officer;
- high-value or high-risk matters; or
- any workflow requiring maker-checker separation.

## Competent authority acting as verifier

The competent authority may also act as verifier only where the approved workflow combines verification and final decision at that level.

Where technical verification and administrative approval are different functions, separate officers shall be recorded.

For example:

- a field officer may verify physical completion;
- an accounts officer may verify financial scrutiny;
- a technical officer may verify specifications;
- the competent authority may thereafter approve, accept, reject or return the matter.

Verification shall confirm facts or compliance. Approval shall record the competent decision. The two stages shall not be silently merged where separate scrutiny is required.

## Same-person restrictions

The same person should not ordinarily perform all of the following for one action:

- issue the action;
- execute the action;
- submit progress;
- verify the progress;
- approve the result; and
- finally close the action.

For routine and low-risk workflows, a limited combination of roles may be permitted by configuration.

For high-risk workflows, the system shall enforce maker-checker or maker-checker-approver separation.

The workflow configuration shall identify which combinations are:

- permitted;
- permitted with recorded reason;
- permitted only for low-risk matters;
- prohibited; or
- subject to separate approval.

## Functional verification

Verification authority shall follow the subject of the action.

### Engineering verification

Engineering progress or technical compliance shall be verified by an officer having the required engineering jurisdiction and competence over the project, Section, structure, item or subject.

### Inspection verification

Inspection compliance shall be verified by the assigned inspecting officer, authorised superior officer or another officer specifically assigned to verify the observation.

### Financial and audit verification

Bills, estimates, tenders, accounts, expenditure, recoveries and audit compliance shall be verified or scrutinised by the assigned Auditor, Divisional Accountant, accounts personnel or competent financial authority, as applicable.

An engineering officer’s project authority shall not automatically provide financial-verification authority.

### Correspondence verification

Compliance arising from correspondence may be verified by the issuing officer, concerned processing officer, Project Officer, Personal Assistant or competent authority according to the assigned workflow and subject jurisdiction.

### Statutory and specialised verification

Legal, land-acquisition, Railway, forest, utility, environmental, safety or other specialised compliance may require a designated specialist or separately authorised officer.

The system shall permit a specialist verifier through an explicit functional assignment without giving that verifier unrestricted access to unrelated project records.

## Project Officers and Personal Assistants

A Project Officer may verify or process actions only for the Divisions, Subdivisions, projects or subjects expressly assigned to that Project Officer and only where the functional assignment includes the relevant permission.

A Personal Assistant may process, coordinate, forward or place a matter before the Head of Office. A Personal Assistant may verify only where independent verification authority is expressly delegated and recorded.

Acting on behalf of the Head of Office shall not automatically make the Personal Assistant the competent authority. The system shall distinguish between:

- processed by;
- examined by;
- verified by;
- recommended by;
- submitted on behalf of;
- approved by; and
- decided by.

## Auditors and accounts personnel

Auditors and accounts personnel may be selected as verifiers for financial, contractual, tender, bill, account or scrutiny matters falling within their recorded assignment.

They shall not be selected to verify general engineering execution unless separately authorised for that purpose.

The Divisional Accountant may supervise the audit and accounts workflow of the Division but shall not automatically become the competent authority for engineering or administrative decisions.

## Cross-office and specialist verification

A verifier or competent authority from another Subdivision, Division, Circle or Regional Office may be used only through:

- superior-office jurisdiction;
- an active additional-charge assignment;
- deputation or temporary duty;
- a specialist verification assignment;
- an approved audit assignment;
- an approved technical-assistance assignment;
- an authorised cross-office project grant;
- a competent-authority order; or
- another approved and auditable exception.

The exception shall record:

- officer;
- originating and receiving offices;
- project or subject;
- verification or authority role;
- reason;
- approving authority;
- order or reference;
- effective start and end dates;
- permissions;
- whether final approval is included;
- review or expiry status; and
- audit history.

Cross-office visibility alone shall not permit verification or approval.

## External verifier or authority

An external consultant, Independent Engineer, Authority’s Engineer, laboratory, third-party expert, statutory authority or other external entity may be recorded as an external verifier where contract conditions or official procedure require it.

External verification shall be separately identified from internal departmental verification.

The record shall include:

- organisation;
- authorised representative;
- role;
- contract or authority basis;
- project or subject;
- scope of verification;
- report or certificate reference;
- date;
- attachment;
- validity;
- internal officer responsible for accepting the external verification; and
- final competent authority.

An external verifier shall not receive internal application access beyond the separately approved external-user or submission mechanism.

## Verification outcome

A verifier shall be able to record an outcome such as:

- verified;
- partly verified;
- not verified;
- returned for clarification;
- evidence insufficient;
- site verification pending;
- correction required;
- recommendation for approval;
- recommendation for rejection; or
- another approved outcome.

The verifier shall record:

- verified percentage or quantity, where applicable;
- observations;
- evidence examined;
- site-visit details, where applicable;
- differences from the submitted claim;
- deficiencies;
- required correction;
- recommendation;
- date and time; and
- supporting attachment or reference.

Submitted progress shall remain claimed progress until verification is completed.

Only verified progress shall be treated as official progress for reports, dashboards, completion assessment and escalation.

## Partial verification

The verifier may accept less progress than claimed.

The system shall separately preserve:

- claimed progress;
- verified progress;
- rejected or unverified portion;
- verification remarks;
- evidence;
- verifier;
- date; and
- subsequent resubmission.

A lower verified value shall not overwrite or erase the original submitted claim.

## Reverification and correction

Where progress is resubmitted after correction, the system shall create a new verification event rather than overwrite the earlier verification.

A verified record may be reopened only by an authorised officer and with a recorded reason.

Correction of a mistaken verification shall preserve:

- original verification;
- correcting officer;
- reason;
- previous and corrected values;
- authority for correction;
- date and time; and
- audit history.

## Competent-authority decision

After verification, the competent authority may:

- approve;
- accept;
- partly approve;
- reject;
- return for correction;
- seek further verification;
- seek additional evidence;
- extend time;
- transfer jurisdiction;
- close the action;
- reopen the action; or
- take another authorised decision.

The competent authority’s decision shall be distinct from the verifier’s recommendation.

The competent authority may disagree with the verifier but shall record reasons for doing so.

Where the competent authority approves without the required verification, the system shall block the action or require a formally approved exception, depending on the workflow.

## Delegated competent authority

Where a superior officer delegates authority, the delegation shall separately record:

- delegating authority;
- delegated officer;
- office and post;
- project, contract, subject or monetary scope;
- powers delegated;
- powers excluded;
- effective start date;
- effective end date;
- order or reference;
- conditions;
- whether further delegation is allowed;
- status; and
- audit history.

The system shall not infer delegation merely because the superior officer is absent or the subordinate is the next officer in rank.

## Temporary absence and substitute authority

Where the normal verifier or competent authority is unavailable, the system shall not permit selection of any convenient user.

A substitute shall require:

- an active additional-charge, temporary-duty or substitute assignment;
- approved order or authority;
- defined scope;
- effective dates; and
- specific verification or approval permission.

Pending actions shall be shown to the authorised substitute only during the effective assignment period.

## Conflict of interest

A user shall not act as verifier or competent authority where the user:

- submitted the progress;
- directly executed the work requiring independent verification;
- has a personal or organisational conflict;
- is the subject of the audit observation;
- made the administrative change under review;
- is verifying their own access or assignment;
- is approving their own entitlement;
- is correcting their own high-risk administrative action without independent review; or
- is otherwise prohibited by the approved workflow.

Where a potential conflict exists, the system shall require another authorised verifier or competent authority.

## Platform Administrator separation

Platform Administrator context shall not grant operational verification, approval or competent-authority powers.

A Platform Administrator may configure:

- authority categories;
- verification roles;
- workflow stages;
- permission codes;
- assignment records;
- delegated-authority records;
- office hierarchy;
- project jurisdiction;
- validation rules; and
- audit reports.

To verify or approve an operational action, the user shall return to an authorised Officer context.

A Platform Administrator shall not be selectable as verifier merely because the account has administrator entitlement.

## Authorized dropdowns

Verifier and competent-authority dropdowns shall be generated dynamically from the current action context.

The dropdown shall show only users who satisfy all relevant requirements, including:

- active account;
- approved status;
- active assignment;
- correct Officer context;
- valid office or project scope;
- required functional post;
- required permission;
- effective dates;
- appropriate workflow stage;
- absence of conflict;
- required separation from submitter;
- monetary or technical limit, where applicable; and
- any required delegation or exception.

The interface should display the basis of eligibility, such as:

- office;
- designation;
- functional post;
- project scope;
- verification role;
- competent-authority category;
- delegation reference; and
- assignment validity.

The system shall not expose a broad unrestricted user list.

## Database and API enforcement

Verifier and competent-authority rules shall be enforced in the backend and database, not merely through the visible dropdown.

Before setting or changing `action_items.verifier_id` or `action_items.competent_authority_id`, the system shall validate:

- acting user;
- active Officer context;
- action scope;
- selected officer’s active assignment;
- selected officer’s jurisdiction;
- selected officer’s permission;
- reporting or functional relationship;
- workflow stage;
- separation from submitter;
- conflict-of-interest rules;
- delegation or authority reference;
- effective dates;
- limits, where applicable; and
- absence of suspension, expiry or revocation.

A direct API request attempting to assign an unauthorised verifier or competent authority shall be rejected.

## Assignment changes

When a verifier or competent authority:

- transfers;
- retires;
- is suspended;
- loses jurisdiction;
- changes functional post;
- has an assignment expire;
- has authority revoked; or
- becomes unavailable,

the system shall review all pending actions assigned to that role.

The system shall identify:

- actions awaiting verification;
- actions awaiting approval;
- partly verified actions;
- returned actions;
- overdue decisions;
- delegated matters;
- cross-office assignments; and
- unresolved high-risk actions.

The competent office shall reassign each pending responsibility through an audited process.

Historical verification and approval events shall continue to show the officer, office, assignment and authority existing at the time of the event.

## Audit requirements

Every selection, change, verification, recommendation, approval, rejection, return, reassignment, reopening or correction shall record:

- action identifier;
- acting user;
- active Officer context;
- acting assignment;
- submitter;
- verifier;
- competent authority;
- previous and new verifier;

### 14. Reconcile legacy and current action-assignment models

**Question requiring confirmation:** Should legacy `action_items.assigned_to` be retained, backfilled into `action_assignees`, or retired after parity is demonstrated?

**Why this matters:** Current UI and policies use the two models differently. Introducing hierarchy enforcement before reconciliation can create conflicting mutation authority.

**Available options:**

1. Make `action_assignees` authoritative, backfill legacy values, retain `assigned_to` temporarily as read-only compatibility data, then retire it.
2. Keep both models synchronized permanently.
3. Return to one assignee per action.

**Codex recommendation:** Make `action_assignees` authoritative. Additively backfill every valid legacy assignee, compare behavior, freeze legacy writes, and remove or deprecate `assigned_to` only after policy and rollback tests pass.

**Affected areas:** `action_items.assigned_to`, `action_assignees`, progress/update RLS, Action Register, Action Detail, notifications, Dashboard, migration/backfill report, and rollback scripts.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt `action_assignees` as the authoritative action-assignment model. The legacy field `action_items.assigned_to` shall be treated only as temporary compatibility data during migration and shall not remain a second independent source of authority.

## Authoritative model

Every action assignment shall be represented through `action_assignees`.

The authoritative assignment model shall support:

- one lead assignee;
- one or more supporting assignees;
- verifier;
- competent authority;
- information recipients;
- assignment status;
- assignment type;
- effective dates;
- responsibility share, where applicable;
- action-receipt permission;
- reassignment history; and
- audit history.

The field `action_items.assigned_to` shall not independently determine:

- who may view the action;
- who may submit progress;
- who is responsible for completion;
- who receives notifications;
- who may forward or reassign the action;
- who may verify the action; or
- who appears as the current assignee.

All such behavior shall be derived from the active rows in `action_assignees` together with the approved office, project, hierarchy and permission rules.

## Legacy backfill

Every valid non-null value in `action_items.assigned_to` shall be reviewed and additively backfilled into `action_assignees`.

The migration shall:

1. identify every action containing a legacy assignee;
2. check whether the same user already exists in `action_assignees`;
3. create a missing active assignee row without duplicating an existing assignment;
4. classify the migrated user as lead assignee unless reliable existing data proves another role;
5. preserve the original action identifier and legacy value;
6. record the migration source as `legacy_assigned_to`;
7. preserve creation and update dates where technically reliable;
8. record the migration batch and timestamp;
9. flag invalid, missing, inactive or ambiguous users for review; and
10. produce a complete before-and-after migration report.

No existing valid `action_assignees` row shall be deleted or overwritten merely because a legacy `assigned_to` value exists.

## Conflict handling

Where `action_items.assigned_to` and `action_assignees` identify different users, the system shall not silently choose one.

Each conflict shall be classified, for example, as:

- legacy user missing from current assignees;
- current assignee missing from legacy field;
- multiple current assignees with one legacy user;
- inactive or deleted legacy user;
- invalid user identifier;
- duplicate assignment;
- lead-assignee ambiguity;
- completed action with historical inconsistency; or
- another unresolved mismatch.

For active actions, the concerned authorised officer shall review the conflict and confirm:

- lead assignee;
- supporting assignees;
- responsibility status;
- effective date;
- whether reassignment occurred;
- whether notifications must be regenerated; and
- whether any pending progress or verification responsibility is affected.

Historical actions shall preserve the original legacy value for audit, even after the authoritative assignment is corrected.

## Temporary compatibility period

During the migration period, `action_items.assigned_to` may remain available only for controlled compatibility purposes.

It may be used for:

- migration comparison;
- rollback verification;
- identifying old application behavior;
- historical audit display;
- temporary support for a legacy read path not yet converted; and
- reconciliation reporting.

It shall not be used for new operational authorization.

New application code, database policies, reports and notifications shall read assignments from `action_assignees`.

## Freeze legacy writes

After the backfill is completed and validated, normal application writes to `action_items.assigned_to` shall be frozen.

The system shall prevent:

- new actions from relying only on `assigned_to`;
- reassignment through `assigned_to`;
- progress authorization through `assigned_to`;
- notification routing through `assigned_to`;
- direct API updates that alter only `assigned_to`; and
- database functions from treating `assigned_to` as the current assignee.

Where temporary synchronization is required during transition, it shall be one-way and controlled:

`action_assignees` → compatibility value in `action_items.assigned_to`

The legacy field shall never overwrite or reverse the authoritative `action_assignees` records.

## Compatibility value

If a temporary compatibility value must continue to be populated, it shall represent only the current lead assignee.

Where an action has:

- one lead assignee and supporting assignees, `assigned_to` may mirror the lead assignee;
- multiple supporting assignees but no lead assignee, the action shall be flagged for correction rather than selecting one arbitrarily;
- no active assignee, `assigned_to` shall remain null;
- a transferred lead assignee, the compatibility value may be updated only through the authoritative reassignment process; or
- a completed or closed historical action, the retained value shall not be rewritten unless necessary to correct invalid data through an audited process.

The compatibility value shall carry no independent legal or operational meaning.

## Action creation

Every new action shall create one or more `action_assignees` rows as part of the same controlled transaction.

Action creation shall identify:

- lead assignee;
- supporting assignees, where any;
- assignment role;
- assignment basis;
- assigned project or subject;
- assigning officer;
- reporting relationship;
- effective date;
- due date;
- permitted operations;
- whether progress submission is allowed;
- whether forwarding is allowed;
- whether action receipt is allowed; and
- audit record.

An action shall not be considered successfully issued if the required assignee rows are not created.

## Multiple assignees

The authoritative model shall permit multiple assignees.

Each assignee row shall separately identify the person’s role, such as:

- lead assignee;
- supporting assignee;
- joint-responsibility assignee;
- processing officer;
- information recipient;
- verifier;
- competent authority; or
- another approved role.

The system shall not treat all assignees as equally responsible.

For joint responsibility:

- one lead assignee should ordinarily be identified;
- each supporting assignee’s responsibility should be stated;
- progress may be submitted jointly or separately as configured;
- verification shall remain distinct;
- completion shall depend on the configured workflow; and
- the audit history shall show each person’s contribution and responsibility.

## Reassignment

All reassignment shall be performed through `action_assignees`.

A reassignment shall record:

- previous assignee;
- new assignee;
- previous and new role;
- reason;
- assigning authority;
- active Officer context;
- effective date and time;
- remaining due date;
- transferred responsibilities;
- retained supporting assignees;
- pending progress;
- pending verification;
- notification status; and
- audit history.

The previous assignee shall remain visible in history but shall cease to have active responsibility unless retained in another active role.

The system shall not implement reassignment by merely replacing `action_items.assigned_to`.

## Progress submission

Progress submission authority shall be based on an active `action_assignees` row and the permission attached to that assignment.

A user may submit progress only where the user:

- is an active lead or supporting assignee;
- has the required progress-submission permission;
- has valid Officer context;
- remains within effective assignment dates;
- has not been suspended or revoked;
- has scope over the action; and
- is permitted by the workflow stage.

A value in `action_items.assigned_to` shall not by itself permit progress submission.

## Notifications

Action notifications shall be generated from authoritative assignments.

The system shall notify, as applicable:

- lead assignee;
- supporting assignees;
- verifier;
- competent authority;
- issuer;
- reporting officer;
- information recipients; and
- any specifically configured escalation recipient.

Notifications shall not be routed solely from `action_items.assigned_to`.

When assignments change, notification recipients shall be recalculated from current active assignments while preserving historical notification records.

## Dashboard and Action Register

The Dashboard, Action Register and Action Detail shall display assignment data from `action_assignees`.

They shall show, where applicable:

- lead assignee;
- supporting assignees;
- assignment role;
- responsibility status;
- assignment date;
- reassignment history;
- progress-submission rights;
- verification role; and
- current responsible office.

During migration, the interface may show a warning where legacy and current assignment data do not match.

## Direct URLs, APIs and RLS

All direct URLs, APIs, database functions and RLS policies shall use `action_assignees` as the authoritative assignment source.

The backend shall validate:

- current active assignment;
- assignment role;
- effective date;
- project and office scope;
- reporting relationship;
- permitted operation;
- suspension or revocation;
- selected Officer context; and
- workflow stage.

A direct API request relying only on `action_items.assigned_to` shall not receive authorization.

## Completed and historical actions

Historical integrity shall be preserved.

For completed, closed, cancelled or archived actions:

- existing legacy values shall be retained for audit;
- authoritative historical assignee rows shall be created where reliably possible;
- uncertain historical mappings shall be marked as inferred or unresolved;
- original values shall not be silently rewritten;
- progress, verification and workflow events shall continue to reference the persons recorded at the time; and
- current access shall not be granted merely because a user appears in historical assignment data.

Historical assignee rows shall be distinguishable from currently active responsibilities.

## Migration validation

Before legacy writes are frozen, the system shall validate at least:

- every non-null legacy assignee;
- every active action;
- every action with no current assignee row;
- every action with multiple assignees;
- every action with conflicting assignee data;
- every progress update;
- every open verification;
- every pending competent-authority decision;
- every Action Register entry;
- every Action Detail view;
- every Dashboard action item;
- every notification rule;
- every report and export;
- every direct URL;
- every relevant RLS policy; and
- every action-related database function.

The validation report shall show:

- total actions;
- actions with legacy assignees;
- actions with authoritative assignee rows;
- successful backfills;
- duplicate rows avoided;
- invalid users;
- missing users;
- conflicting assignees;
- actions without lead assignees;
- actions with multiple lead assignees;
- progress updates lacking valid assignees;
- notifications affected;
- unresolved records; and
- rollback readiness.

## Parity period

A controlled parity period shall be completed before retirement of the legacy field.

During the parity period, the system shall compare old and new behavior for:

- action visibility;
- assignee display;
- progress submission;
- forwarding;
- reassignment;
- verification;
- notifications;
- Dashboard counts;
- reports;
- exports;
- direct links;
- search results; and
- API authorization.

Any difference shall be investigated and classified as:

- intended improvement;
- legacy defect;
- migration defect;
- data-quality issue;
- missing assignment;
- policy mismatch; or
- unresolved risk.

The legacy field shall not be retired until all material differences are reviewed.

## Rollback

Migration shall be additive and reversible until parity is confirmed.

The rollback plan shall preserve:

- original `action_items.assigned_to` values;
- migration batch identifiers;
- inserted `action_assignees` rows;
- source markers;
- before-and-after reports;
- unresolved conflicts;
- synchronization status; and
- rollback scripts.

Rollback shall not delete valid pre-existing `action_assignees` rows.

Any rollback script shall remove or deactivate only rows created by the identified migration batch and shall preserve audit history.

## Retirement criteria

The field `action_items.assigned_to` may be deprecated or removed only after all of the following are confirmed:

1. every valid legacy assignee is backfilled or formally resolved;
2. all active actions have valid authoritative assignments;
3. all action UI modules use `action_assignees`;
4. all progress and workflow permissions use `action_assignees`;
5. all notifications use `action_assignees`;
6. all reports and exports use `action_assignees`;
7. all direct URLs and APIs enforce `action_assignees`;
8. all RLS policies use `action_assignees`;
9. parity testing passes;
10. rollback testing passes;
11. unresolved records are either corrected or formally accepted;
12. migration reports are retained;
13. no production process writes to `assigned_to`; and
14. final retirement is approved through the controlled migration process.

## Deprecation approach

The preferred sequence shall be:

1. audit both models;
2. backfill missing authoritative assignments;
3. resolve conflicts;
4. convert reads to `action_assignees`;
5. convert writes to `action_assignees`;
6. stop independent legacy writes;
7. retain optional lead-assignee compatibility mirroring;
8. complete parity testing;
9. remove legacy dependencies;
10. mark `assigned_to` deprecated;
11. retain the field temporarily if rollback requires it;
12. remove the field only in a later reviewed schema migration.

The field shall not be dropped in the same migration that first introduces authoritative assignment enforcement.

## Administrative correction

Administrative correction shall record:

- action;
- previous assignment data;
- corrected assignment data;
- reason;
- source evidence;
- approving authority;
- migration or correction batch;
- date and time;
- affected progress or workflow records;
- notification impact; and
- validation result.

Platform Administrator context shall not itself provide operational responsibility for the action.

## Audit requirements

Every migration, assignment creation, reassignment, role change, deactivation, correction, synchronization, conflict resolution, deprecation step and rollback shall record:

- action identifier;

## Special-record and external-access decisions

### 15. Define access for external users, consultants, contractors, and agencies

**Question requiring confirmation:** Which external identities may sign in, which modules and operations may they access, and how are agency affiliation, project scope, expiry, and sponsorship recorded?

**Why this matters:** Consultant and contractor labels already exist, but no verified external-user authorization model was found. Office hierarchy should not accidentally give external users inherited government-office scope.

**Available options:**

1. Explicit, time-bounded project access with a named internal sponsor and narrowly defined operations.
2. Office-based access like internal officers.
3. No external login; exchange information outside MARGDARSHI.

**Codex recommendation:** If external login is required, use explicit project grants only, with agency, sponsor, purpose, dates, and permitted operations. Grant no office-descendant inheritance, platform administration, broad search/reporting, or cross-project attachment access.

**Affected areas:** `profiles`, canonical user/post types, possible agency master, `project_user_access`, action receipt, correspondence, inspections, attachments, directory exposure, RLS tests, and offboarding/revocation.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt an explicit, sponsored and time-bounded external-access model. External consultants, contractors, concessionaires, testing agencies, utility agencies and other authorised organisations may be permitted to sign in only where their participation is necessary for an approved project or official workflow.

External users shall not be treated as government officers and shall not receive access through the Regional Office, Circle, Division, Subdivision or Section hierarchy.

### 16. Define visibility for records without a project

**Question requiring confirmation:** How should correspondence and calendar events with null `project_id` be authorized, and should new operational records be allowed without a project?

**Why this matters:** Project-derived policies cannot determine scope for a null-project record. A permissive fallback could expose such records globally.

**Available options:**

1. Require a project for new operational records; restrict legacy unlinked rows to creator plus approved office scope until classified.
2. Scope null-project records to the creator's active office.
3. Restrict them to Platform Administrators.
4. Make them visible to all authenticated users.

**Codex recommendation:** Require a project for new project-operational correspondence and events wherever practical. For genuinely administrative unlinked records, store an explicit owning office. Until migration is complete, allow only the creator and a narrowly authorized administrator to view unresolved legacy rows; never use global visibility as fallback.

**Affected areas:** `correspondence.project_id`, `calendar_events.project_id`, potential owning-office field, forms, Dashboard, Calendar, Search, Reports, direct routes, RLS fallback policy, data-classification migration, and zero-scope tests.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt a controlled dual-scope model. Every new record shall be linked either to:

1. an approved project or defined project portion; or
2. an explicit owning office and approved non-project record category.

A null `project_id` shall never create global visibility.

## General rule

Project-operational correspondence, calendar events, actions, inspections, submissions, compliance records, project meetings, technical reviews and other project-dependent records shall ordinarily require an approved project reference.

Records that genuinely concern office administration rather than a particular project may be created without a project only where they contain an explicit owning office and an approved administrative or general-subject classification.

The system shall therefore distinguish between:

- project-linked operational records;
- office-owned administrative records;
- multi-project or programme-level records;
- inter-office coordination records;
- confidential office records;
- personal or establishment records; and
- unresolved legacy records.

A record shall not be treated as organisation-wide merely because `project_id` is null.

## Mandatory scope source

Every new correspondence or calendar record shall have at least one authoritative scope source:

- `project_id`;
- defined project-package, section, chainage or subject relationship;
- `owning_office_id`;
- approved multi-project group;
- approved programme or scheme;
- approved inter-office scope;
- user-specific confidential scope; or

Sibling offices shall not receive access unless the record is expressly shared or an approved assignment exists.

## Office hierarchy visibility

Visibility for office-owned records shall follow the approved hierarchy:

`Regional Office → Circle Office → Division Office → Subdivision Office → Section`

A user may view an office-owned record only where at least one of the following applies:

- the record belongs to the user’s selected active office;
- the record belongs to a descendant office within the user’s approved jurisdiction;
- the user has a functional assignment covering that office or subject;
- the record is expressly shared with the user or the user’s office;
- the user is the creator and still retains authorised access;
- the user is an authorised recipient;
- the user is the designated processing, audit, verification or approving officer; or
- a separate time-bounded explicit grant exists.

Office visibility shall not arise merely from designation or seniority.

## Creator access

The creator of a record shall not permanently receive unrestricted access merely because the user created it.

Creator access shall continue only where:

- the creator’s active assignment still covers the record;
- the creator remains a participant in the workflow;
- the record is still within the creator’s authorised office or project scope;
- historical read access is approved; or
- an explicit continuing grant exists.

Where the creator changes office or loses jurisdiction, the system shall preserve creator identity in history but shall recalculate current access.

## Administrative records and Platform Administrator context

Platform Administrator context shall not automatically provide access to the full contents of all non-project records.

Platform Administrators may access configuration metadata necessary for system administration, such as:

- record identifier;
- owning office;
- category;
- scope configuration;
- access rule;
- creator identifier;
- record status;
- retention status;
- security classification;
- attachment count;
- audit metadata; and
- data-quality warnings.

Operational or confidential content shall remain subject to the user’s Officer context or a separately approved administrative-support procedure.

Platform Administrator context shall not become a fallback reader for every null-project record.

## Correspondence without a project

New correspondence may omit `project_id` only where it is classified as an approved office-owned or general-subject communication.

The correspondence shall then contain:

- owning office;
- direction;
- sender;
- recipient;
- subject;
- correspondence category;
- confidentiality level;
- responsible officer;
- processing office;
- linked office or offices;
- authority reference;
- date;
- status;
- disposal responsibility; and
- access classification.

Examples may include:

- general departmental circulars;
- establishment correspondence;
- office administration;
- audit programmes;
- general policy instructions;
- staff-related official communication;
- non-project legal matters;
- office infrastructure;
- user-access matters; and
- office-level financial administration.

Project-related correspondence shall not be classified as general merely to avoid selecting a project.

## Calendar events without a project

A calendar event may omit `project_id` where it is genuinely an office, establishment, administrative, training, audit or general review event.

Such an event shall contain:

- owning office;
- event category;
- organiser;
- responsible officer;
- participating offices;
- invited users;
- confidentiality level;
- start and end date and time;
- location or meeting mode;
- recurrence rule, where applicable;
- authority or agenda reference; and
- visibility rule.

Examples may include:

- office meeting;
- staff review;
- Circle or Division review meeting;
- audit programme;
- training;
- holiday;
- administrative deadline;
- office inspection;
- system-maintenance event;
- establishment review; or
- jurisdictional coordination meeting.

A project review, site visit, milestone, contractor meeting or project deadline shall require project linkage.

## Multi-project and programme records

A record concerning several projects shall not use null `project_id` as a substitute for proper scope.

The system shall support a separate multi-project or programme relationship, such as:

- highway-wide review;
- Division-wide project review;
- Circle-level programme;
- Regional Office review;
- land-acquisition review across packages;
- safety campaign;
- encroachment drive;
- plantation programme;
- court-compliance drive;
- audit programme;
- monsoon preparedness review; or
- another approved grouped activity.

Such a record shall identify:

- owning office;
- included projects;
- included offices;
- subject;
- responsible officer;
- permitted audience;
- confidentiality level;
- effective period;
- authority reference; and
- audit history.

Users shall see only the portions falling within their approved jurisdiction unless a broader explicit grant exists.

## Inter-office correspondence

Inter-office records shall identify:

- originating office;
- receiving office or offices;
- owning office;
- responsible officer;
- processing office;
- subject classification;
- project relationship, where any;
- confidentiality;
- action required;
- due date;
- disposal office; and
- audit history.

Visibility shall be limited to:

- authorised users in the originating office;
- authorised users in the receiving office;
- superior offices having approved jurisdiction;
- named processing or approving officers; and
- separately authorised participants.

Other offices shall not receive access merely because they belong to the same Division, Circle or Regional Office.

## Confidential and personal records

Certain non-project records shall require stricter visibility, including:

- employee personal information;
- disciplinary matters;
- confidential legal advice;
- vigilance matters;
- security incidents;
- user-access investigations;
- confidential audit observations;
- sealed tender matters;
- commercially sensitive records;
- personal medical or service records; and
- legally privileged communications.

Such records shall use a named-recipient or restricted-role model rather than broad office inheritance.

The system shall record:

- confidentiality classification;
- authorised roles;
- authorised users;
- owning office;
- approval authority;
- review date;
- retention requirement; and
- access history.

A senior-office user shall not automatically receive access unless the user’s functional assignment includes the relevant confidential jurisdiction.

## Legacy null-project records

Existing records with null `project_id` shall be treated as unresolved until classified.

They shall not be made visible to all authenticated users.

During migration, each legacy record shall be assigned one of the following:

- linked to an existing project;
- linked to multiple projects;
- linked to an owning office;
- classified as an administrative record;
- classified as confidential;
- linked to a programme or general subject;
- archived as historical;
- marked invalid or duplicate; or
- retained as unresolved pending manual review.

## Temporary legacy access

Until classification is complete, an unresolved legacy record shall be visible only to:

- the original creator, where still authorised;
- named sender or recipient users, where identifiable;
- the owning office inferred from reliable existing data;
- the concerned Head of Office;
- a specifically authorised migration reviewer; and
- a Platform Administrator only through a restricted data-correction or migration-support process.

An unresolved legacy record shall not appear in:

- general Dashboard counts;
- broad search;
- global reports;
- unrestricted exports;
- general calendar views;
- unrelated correspondence registers; or
- user notifications outside the authorised review group.

## Legacy classification process

The migration process shall review every null-project record and capture:

- record identifier;
- record type;
- creator;
- creation date;
- subject;
- current sender and recipient;
- possible project;
- possible owning office;
- attachment classification;
- current visible users;
- proposed classification;
- proposed access rule;
- reviewing officer;
- evidence used;
- approval status;
- migration batch;
- unresolved issues; and
- audit history.

Where project linkage can be reliably inferred from:

- project code;
- highway number;
- package name;
- letter subject;
- attachment metadata;
- sender or recipient;
- action relationship;
- inspection relationship;
- chainage;
- contract reference; or
- another reliable field,

the record may be proposed for project linkage, but the change shall be reviewed before becoming authoritative.

## Ambiguous records

Where a legacy record could relate to more than one project or office, the system shall not select one arbitrarily.

The record shall be flagged for manual classification.

Until resolution:

- visibility shall remain restricted;
- no new action shall be issued from the record unless authorised;
- no broad export shall include it;
- attachments shall remain protected;
- direct URL access shall enforce the restricted scope; and
- the unresolved status shall be shown to authorised reviewers.

## New record validation

Before creating correspondence or a calendar event, the application shall ask the user to select the record basis:

- project-linked;
- multi-project;
- office administrative;
- inter-office;
- confidential;
- general programme; or
- another approved category.

Based on the selection, the form shall require the applicable scope fields.

The backend shall reject creation where:

- a project-operational category lacks a project;
- an administrative category lacks an owning office;
- a multi-project category lacks included projects;
- an inter-office record lacks originating or receiving offices;
- a confidential record lacks an approved access list;
- the user lacks scope over the selected project or office;
- the selected category is incompatible with the chosen scope; or
- no authoritative visibility source exists.

## Category catalogue

MARGDARSHI shall maintain an approved record-category catalogue.

Each category shall define:

- category code;
- display name;
- whether project linkage is mandatory;
- whether owning office is mandatory;
- whether multiple projects are allowed;
- whether multiple offices are allowed;
- default confidentiality;
- permitted creators;
- permitted viewers;
- permitted processors;
- retention rule;
- attachment rules;
- reporting rules;
- search visibility; and
- audit requirements.

Users shall not use unrestricted free text to create new authorization categories.

## Action creation from unlinked records

An action may be created from a non-project correspondence record only where:

- the action is administrative or office-related;
- the issuing user has authority over the recipient;
- both users share the relevant office or subject jurisdiction;
- the action category permits non-project actions;
- the record has an owning office;
- competent authority and verifier rules are satisfied; and
- the action does not concern hidden project work.

A project-operational action shall require project linkage even if its source correspondence was previously unlinked.

## Search behavior

Search results shall use the same authoritative scope rules as the source record.

A user shall not discover an unauthorised null-project record through:

- subject text;
- sender name;
- recipient name;
- attachment filename;
- calendar title;
- search suggestions;
- project code;
- direct identifier; or
- recent-history lists.

Search shall return only records that the user is authorised to open.

## Dashboard and register behavior

Dashboard counts and registers shall exclude unresolved records outside the user’s authorised scope.

Office-owned records may appear in:

- office correspondence summary;
- administrative calendar;
- office action register;
- audit review;
- pending disposal summary; and
- office-level report,

only where the user has approved jurisdiction.

Project dashboards shall not include administrative records unless intentionally linked.

## Reports and exports

Reports shall separate:

- project-linked records;
- multi-project records;
- office-owned administrative records;
- confidential records;
- unresolved legacy records; and
- archived records.

Exports shall enforce office, project, category and confidentiality restrictions.

A user shall not export all null-project records merely because the records have no project relationship.

## Attachments

Attachments belonging to non-project records shall inherit access from the parent record.

The system shall validate:

- parent-record access;
- owning office;
- category;
- confidentiality level;
- active assignment;
- permitted operation;
- effective dates;
- named-recipient restrictions; and
- revocation status.

An attachment shall not become publicly accessible merely because its parent record has null `project_id`.

Storage paths, signed URLs and download links shall not bypass record-level scope.

## Notifications

Notifications for office-owned records shall be limited to:

- named recipients;
- authorised users in the owning office;
- designated processing officers;
- reporting officers;
- approving authorities;
- auditors or reviewers specifically assigned; and
- users with an explicit grant.

Users in unrelated offices shall not receive notifications.

## Retention and archival

Non-project records shall retain:

- owning office at the time of creation;
- creator context;
- recipients;
- classification;
- access history;
- processing history;
- disposal decision;
- archive status; and
- audit trail.

If the owning office is reorganised, historical records shall preserve the original office context while current access is recalculated through the approved reorganisation rules.

Archival shall not automatically make a record visible more broadly.

## Direct API and RLS enforcement

The database and backend shall enforce the same rules as the interface.

For every null-project record request, the system shall verify:

- authenticated user;
- selected active Officer context;
- owning office;
- office ancestry;
- functional assignment;
- record category;
- named recipient or participant;
- confidentiality classification;
- explicit grant;
- effective dates;
- permitted operation;
- suspension or revocation; and
- unresolved legacy status.

A direct API request shall be rejected where no valid authorization source exists.

No policy shall use “authenticated user” or “approved user” as the only condition for reading a null-project record.

## Default-deny rule

Where neither a valid project relationship nor an approved owning-office or restricted-scope relationship exists, access shall be denied.

The absence of a project shall never be treated as unrestricted scope.

## Administrative correction

A Platform Administrator may correct a missing project or owning-office relationship only through an audited data-correction process.

The correction shall record:

- affected record;
- previous scope;
- new project or owning office;
- category;
- reason;
- supporting evidence;
- approving authority;
- affected users;
- before-and-after access comparison;
- attachment impact;
- notification impact;
- success or failure; and
- date and time.

The administrator shall not gain operational access to the record merely by performing the correction.

## Audit reports

The system shall provide reports identifying:

- records with null `project_id`;
- records without owning office;
- records without any valid scope source;
- project-operational records classified as administrative;
- records with conflicting project and office scope;
- unresolved legacy records;
- records visible to unusually broad audiences;
- records with attachments but no valid parent scope;
- records created by inactive users;
- records belonging to inactive offices;
- confidential records with broad office visibility;
- multi-project records without project mappings;
- direct API requests rejected for missing scope;
- records pending classification;
- records whose creator no longer has access;
- records changed during office reorganisation; and
- before-and-after migration access differences.

## Migration sequence

The preferred migration sequence shall be:

1. inventory all null-project correspondence and calendar records;
2. identify creators, senders, recipients and current visibility;
3. infer possible projects and owning offices;
4. classify records by category;
5. restrict unresolved rows;
6. review high-risk or confidential records;
7. add owning-office or multi-project relationships;
8. obtain authorised confirmation;
9. update RLS and backend rules;
10. validate direct URLs, search, reports, exports and attachments;
11. compare before-and-after access;
12. resolve exceptions;
13. preserve rollback data; and
14. prohibit new unscoped records.

## Enforcement consistency

These rules shall be enforced consistently in:

- correspondence creation and editing;
- calendar creation and editing;
- actions created from records;
- Dashboard;
- registers;
- Search;
- Notifications;
- Reports;
- exports;
- attachments;
- direct URLs;
- APIs;
- database functions;
- RLS policies;
- Storage policies;
- migration scripts;
- office reorganisation;
- archival; and
- audit reports.

The approved rule shall therefore be that new project-operational records require project linkage, while genuine non-project administrative records require an explicit owning office and approved classification. Legacy unlinked records shall remain narrowly restricted until they are reviewed and classified. No null-project record shall ever become globally visible by default.

### 17. Define attachment, photograph, and document access inheritance

**Question requiring confirmation:** Must correspondence attachments, inspection photographs, generic attachments, and documents inherit access from their parent record, and are the generic `attachments` and `documents` models in R6.0 scope?

**Why this matters:** Current Storage policies are bucket/role based, and the generic attachment parent is polymorphic without a foreign key. An object path alone is not a safe authorization boundary.

**Available options:**

1. Make every object inherit parent-record/project access; include generic models only after a typed parent strategy is approved.
2. Keep separate bucket-wide role policies.
3. Consolidate all attachment models in R6.0.

**Codex recommendation:** Enforce parent-record inheritance for metadata and Storage SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE. Preserve the two active application flows first. Defer generic `attachments`/`documents` from enforcement or consolidation until their intended use and unambiguous parent relationship are approved.

**Affected areas:** `correspondence` attachment fields, `inspection_photos`, `attachments`, `documents`, private Storage buckets, object paths, Storage policies, project workspace Documents tab, open/download/upload/delete flows, and object inventory migration.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt strict parent-record access inheritance for every uploaded file, attachment, photograph and document. File access shall be derived from the authorised parent record and its project, office, workflow and confidentiality scope. A Storage bucket, object path, filename or application role alone shall never grant access.

For R6.0, preserve and secure the two currently active application flows first:

1. correspondence attachments; and
2. inspection photographs.

The generic `attachments` and `documents` models shall remain outside the first enforcement migration until their intended purpose, parent relationships and lifecycle are confirmed. They shall not be expanded, consolidated or used as a general upload mechanism merely because the tables already exist.

## Core inheritance rule

Every stored object shall have one valid, typed and authoritative parent record.

The parent record shall determine:

- project scope;
- owning office;
- authorised users;
- authorised organisations;
- workflow participants;
- confidentiality classification;
- permitted operations;
- retention requirements;
- effective access dates;
- archival status; and
- deletion restrictions.

A user may access an object only where the user is authorised to access the parent record and has permission for the requested file operation.

Permission to view a parent record shall not automatically include every file operation. Upload, preview, download, replace, supersede and delete permissions shall be evaluated separately.

## Supported parent types in the first R6.0 phase

The initial typed parent relationships shall be:

- correspondence attachment → one `correspondence` record;
- inspection photograph → one inspection record;
- inspection-compliance photograph → the relevant inspection, observation or compliance response, where such a record exists;
- action evidence, when later brought into scope → one action or progress-update record;
- project document, when later approved → one project document register entry.

No object shall be authorised solely through a generic polymorphic text combination such as an unrestricted `parent_type` and `parent_id`.

## Correspondence attachments

Every correspondence attachment shall inherit access from its correspondence record.

The system shall verify:

- access to the correspondence record;
- correspondence project or owning-office scope;
- sender and recipient restrictions;
- confidentiality classification;
- internal or external sharing status;
- user’s active Officer context;
- permitted attachment operation;
- record status;
- attachment status; and
- suspension, expiry or revocation.

An attachment shall not become visible merely because:

- the user can access the same project;
- the user belongs to the same Division;
- the filename appears in search;
- the object path is known;
- the attachment URL was previously copied;
- the user uploaded another attachment to the same bucket; or
- the user has a broad application role such as `editor`.

Where a correspondence record is expressly shared with an external organisation, only the attachments released to that organisation shall be visible. Internal notes, drafts, legal advice, processing documents and restricted attachments shall remain hidden unless separately released.

## Inspection photographs

Every inspection photograph shall inherit access from its inspection record and, where applicable, the relevant observation, compliance submission or verification step.

The system shall distinguish between:

- original inspection photograph;
- contractor or agency compliance photograph;
- consultant observation photograph;
- internal verification photograph;
- before-condition photograph;
- after-condition photograph;
- confidential evidence;
- geotagged evidence;
- superseded photograph; and
- rejected or invalid upload.

A person permitted to submit compliance may upload photographs but shall not thereby receive access to internal inspection evidence, other organisations’ submissions or confidential verification photographs.

A contractor’s upload shall remain marked as submitted evidence until verified by an authorised internal officer. It shall not replace the original inspection photograph or become official verification automatically.

## Typed metadata record

Every stored object shall have a metadata record containing at least:

- immutable file identifier;
- approved parent type;
- parent record identifier;
- project identifier, where applicable;
- owning office identifier, where applicable;
- uploader;
- uploader’s active context;
- external organisation, where applicable;
- original filename;
- safe stored filename or object key;
- file category;
- document type;
- MIME type;
- file size;
- checksum or content hash, where available;
- Storage bucket;
- object path;
- confidentiality classification;
- version number;
- upload date and time;
- effective date, where applicable;
- status;
- current or superseded indicator;
- virus or security scan status, where implemented;
- retention category;
- legal-hold status;
- deletion eligibility;
- source or authority reference;
- remarks; and
- audit history.

The object path shall be treated only as a storage locator and not as the source of authorisation.

## Object path strategy

Storage object paths shall use generated identifiers rather than sensitive record titles or unrestricted user-supplied names.

A path may contain controlled identifiers such as:

- environment;
- approved module;
- parent record identifier;
- file identifier; and
- version identifier.

The path shall not be trusted for access control.

A malformed or manually constructed object path shall not grant access even where it appears to reference an authorised project.

## Storage policy requirements

Private Storage buckets shall remain private.

Storage SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE policies shall verify the related metadata and parent record.

Before allowing an operation, the backend shall validate:

- authenticated user;
- active user status;
- selected active context;
- parent record existence;
- parent record access;
- project or office scope;
- file category;
- operation-specific permission;
- external organisation scope, where applicable;
- effective dates;
- confidentiality classification;
- file status;
- retention or legal-hold restrictions;
- suspension or revocation; and
- record archival status.

A bucket-wide rule based only on `authenticated`, `approved`, `editor`, `admin` or another broad role shall not be sufficient.

## Upload permission

A user may upload a file only where the user:

- can access the parent record;
- has an explicit upload permission for that record type;
- is acting in the appropriate Officer or external-user context;
- is authorised for the relevant project, office or subject;
- is permitted to upload the selected file category;
- is within the effective assignment or grant period; and
- is not suspended or revoked.

The upload form shall not permit the user to manually choose an arbitrary parent identifier outside the user’s scope.

The backend shall independently verify the parent relationship even where the interface has already filtered the available records.

## File validation

Uploads shall be subject to controlled validation, including:

- allowed file types;
- maximum file size;
- filename sanitisation;
- MIME-type verification;
- extension verification;
- duplicate detection, where appropriate;
- malware or virus scanning, where available;
- image metadata handling;
- prohibited executable or script detection;
- content-classification prompt, where required; and
- rejection of empty, corrupt or unsupported files.

The system shall not rely only on the filename extension supplied by the user.

## Photograph metadata

Inspection photographs should record, where available and lawful:

- inspection identifier;
- project;
- chainage;
- latitude and longitude;
- capture date and time;
- upload date and time;
- photographer or submitting user;
- direction or location description;
- observation category;
- before, during, after or compliance stage;
- original or edited status;
- file hash;
- remarks; and
- verification status.

Location and capture metadata supplied by the device shall be treated as supporting evidence and shall not automatically be considered verified.

Any image editing, compression or metadata removal performed by the application shall be recorded where it may affect evidentiary use.

## Preview and download

Preview and download shall be separate permissions.

A user may be allowed to preview a document without receiving unrestricted download permission.

Signed URLs shall:

- be generated only after a current parent-scope check;
- be short-lived;
- be tied to the authorised object;
- avoid exposing permanent public paths;
- expire automatically;
- be regenerated after re-authorisation; and
- cease to work when access is revoked, as far as technically supported.

The application shall not save long-lived signed URLs as permanent record fields.

Copying a previously generated URL shall not provide continuing access after expiry.

## External-user access

External consultants, contractors and agencies shall access only files that fall within their approved project, organisation, workflow and document-category grant.

An external user shall not automatically access:

- every attachment in a project;
- internal correspondence attachments;
- internal inspection photographs;
- another contractor’s submissions;
- confidential drawings;
- tender documents outside the approved scope;
- internal audit records;
- legal advice;
- landowner personal information;
- unrestricted project documents; or
- Storage bucket listings.

External access shall be based on the parent record plus an explicit release or permitted category.

## Upload ownership

The user who uploads a file shall not become the permanent owner of access rights.

The file shall belong to the official parent record and its approved organisational context.

After transfer, expiry, resignation, deactivation or change of assignment:

- uploader identity shall remain in history;
- the file shall remain attached to the official record;
- current access shall be recalculated;
- the uploader shall not retain access merely because the user uploaded it; and
- authorised successor officers shall receive access through the parent record.

## Versioning

Files that may be revised shall support versioning.

A new version shall record:

- prior version;
- new version;
- uploader;
- reason for revision;
- date and time;
- review status;
- whether the earlier version remains valid;
- whether the earlier version is superseded;
- comparison note, where applicable; and
- approval or verification status.

Replacing an object at the same Storage path without a version record shall be prohibited for official records.

Older versions shall not be destroyed merely because a newer version is uploaded, unless an approved retention rule permits removal.

## Superseding and withdrawal

Where a file is corrected, withdrawn or superseded, the system shall retain its history.

The status may include:

- draft;
- submitted;
- under review;
- accepted;
- rejected;
- returned;
- superseded;
- withdrawn;
- archived;
- invalid; or
- deleted under exceptional correction.

A withdrawn or superseded file shall not normally appear as the current file but shall remain visible to authorised users in version or audit history.

## Deletion

Hard deletion shall not be the normal method of removing official attachments or photographs.

The preferred actions shall be:

- deactivate;
- withdraw;
- supersede;
- archive;
- mark invalid;
- detach through a controlled correction; or
- restrict visibility.

Hard deletion may be permitted only where:

- the upload was made in error;
- the object has no valid operational or evidentiary value;
- no approval, action or decision relies on it;
- no legal hold or retention obligation applies;
- dependency checks pass;
- the reason is recorded;
- the authorised approver permits deletion;
- the deletion event is permanently audited; and
- the metadata record is retained where appropriate.

An external user shall not delete a file after it has been formally submitted, acknowledged, reviewed or used in a workflow.

## Retention and legal hold

File retention shall follow the parent record and the approved document category.

Where a parent record is archived, its attachments shall remain protected and archived with it.

A legal hold, court matter, claim, audit, investigation or statutory requirement shall prevent deletion or destructive replacement.

The metadata shall identify whether a file is:

- under legal hold;
- part of an active dispute;
- required for audit;
- required for contractual evidence;
- required for safety evidence;
- required for financial record retention; or
- eligible for routine disposal.

## Parent-record changes

If a parent record’s project, owning office, confidentiality level or jurisdiction changes, file access shall be recalculated.

The system shall not silently preserve all previous file access.

Before a high-risk parent-scope change, the system shall produce a before-and-after report showing:

- affected files;
- existing authorised users;
- proposed authorised users;
- access being added;
- access being removed;
- external users affected;
- confidential objects affected;
- pending signed URLs, where traceable;
- open workflows;
- legal-hold restrictions; and
- unresolved inconsistencies.

Historical audit records shall preserve the scope that existed when each file operation occurred.

## Parent deletion or deactivation

A parent record with attachments shall not be hard deleted without dependency review.

Where a parent record is deactivated or archived:

- attachments shall remain linked;
- upload permissions may cease;
- current access shall follow the archived-record policy;
- direct URLs shall continue to enforce parent access;
- files shall not become orphaned; and
- retention rules shall remain effective.

## Orphan prevention

No object shall exist without a valid metadata record and parent relationship.

The system shall regularly identify:

- Storage objects without metadata;
- metadata without Storage objects;
- attachments with missing parent records;
- attachments with invalid parent types;
- objects in the wrong bucket;
- duplicate object paths;
- files with inconsistent project identifiers;
- files linked to inactive or deleted users;
- files linked to unresolved legacy records;
- files with broad access policies;
- public objects in private workflows;
- long-lived signed URLs stored in records; and
- attachments whose classification conflicts with the parent.

Orphaned or inconsistent objects shall be quarantined from normal access until reviewed.

## Generic `attachments` model

The generic `attachments` table shall not be used for new production enforcement in the first R6.0 phase unless a typed parent design is approved.

Before activation, the following shall be confirmed:

- intended modules;
- allowed parent record types;
- exact foreign-key relationships;
- whether multiple parents are permitted;
- file ownership;
- project and office derivation;
- external-user rules;
- versioning;
- retention;
- deletion;
- Storage bucket;
- object-path convention;
- confidentiality;
- migration source;
- duplicate handling; and
- RLS and Storage policy tests.

An unrestricted `parent_type` text field combined with `parent_id` shall not be accepted as the final authorization model.

## Security and audit decisions

### 18. Decide the disposition of confirmed security findings

**Question requiring confirmation:** Should leaked-password protection be enabled, should `is_margdarshi_admin()` remain directly executable, and when should the confirmed action-progress insert-policy defect be corrected?

**Why this matters:** The audit confirms an exposed `SECURITY DEFINER` helper, inconsistent administrator approval checking, disabled leaked-password protection, and a progress policy that can correlate against the wrong action.

**Available options:**

1. Correct each item in separately reviewed security migrations before broad R6.0 enforcement.
2. Bundle them into the first R6.0 schema/RLS phase.
3. Leave current behavior unchanged.

**Codex recommendation:** Treat these as preconditions to broader rollout. In reviewed changes, enable leaked-password protection subject to Auth impact review; remove unnecessary public/anonymous execute access or redesign the helper with explicit approval/context checks; and fix the progress policy with negative cross-action tests. Keep each change reversible and do not widen another policy to compensate.

**Affected areas:** Supabase Auth settings, `public.is_margdarshi_admin()`, function grants, profile/user-assignment policies, `action_progress_updates` INSERT policy, RLS test harness, Auth regression, and migration rollback.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Treat all confirmed security findings as mandatory preconditions to the broader R6.0 access-control rollout. They shall be corrected through separately reviewed, reversible and tested security changes before new office-, project- and hierarchy-based RLS policies are activated in production.

The security corrections shall not be used as an opportunity to widen another policy, introduce unrelated features or weaken an existing restriction merely to preserve current behaviour.

## General implementation approach

Each confirmed finding shall be handled through a dedicated security work item or clearly separated migration containing:

- current behaviour;
- identified risk;
- proposed correction;
- affected users and modules;
- forward migration;
- rollback procedure;
- positive tests;
- negative tests;
- production-impact assessment;
- deployment order;
- verification steps;
- monitoring plan; and
- audit record.

The changes may be included within the overall R6.0 security phase, but each finding shall remain independently reviewable and reversible.

Production changes shall not be applied directly from an unreviewed SQL editor session. The final SQL, Auth setting changes, function grants and policy changes shall be preserved in version-controlled migration or deployment documentation.

## Leaked-password protection

Enable Supabase leaked-password protection after completing an Auth impact review and confirming that the feature is supported by the current Supabase plan and authentication configuration.

The review shall confirm:

- how existing users are affected;
- whether existing passwords are invalidated or only checked during future password creation or change;
- behaviour during signup;
- behaviour during password reset;
- behaviour during password update;
- error messages shown to users;
- whether OTP or passwordless login is affected;
- administrator recovery procedures;
- support process for users whose chosen password is rejected;
- test-account behaviour; and
- rollback or configuration-reversal procedure.

Leaked-password protection shall be tested in a non-production or controlled test environment before production activation.

Testing shall include:

- signup with an acceptable password;
- signup with a known compromised password;
- password reset with an acceptable password;
- password reset with a compromised password;
- existing-user login;
- OTP login, where used;
- administrator login;
- external-user login, where later enabled;
- user-facing error handling; and
- account-recovery behaviour.

The application shall not disclose unnecessary information about whether a particular password appears in an external breach dataset. The user shall receive a clear instruction to choose a stronger and different password.

Where the application primarily uses OTP or passwordless authentication, leaked-password protection shall still be enabled for any password-based authentication path that remains available.

The production activation date, responsible administrator, test evidence and resulting Auth configuration shall be recorded.

## `is_margdarshi_admin()` security-definer helper

The function `public.is_margdarshi_admin()` shall not remain broadly executable by anonymous users, the public role or unrelated authenticated users merely because it is convenient for RLS.

Its purpose, callers, ownership, execution privileges and security behaviour shall be reviewed before wider R6.0 enforcement.

The review shall identify:

- function definition;
- function owner;
- whether it is `SECURITY DEFINER`;
- configured `search_path`;
- tables and columns read;
- roles currently granted EXECUTE;
- policies and application code that call it;
- whether anonymous execution is possible;
- whether approved-user status is checked;
- whether active Platform Administrator entitlement is checked;
- whether active administrator context is checked;
- whether entitlement effective dates are checked;
- whether suspended or deactivated users are excluded;
- whether results can be manipulated through object shadowing or unsafe search path;
- whether the function reveals information through errors or timing; and
- whether the function is still necessary.

The preferred design shall be a narrowly scoped helper that returns true only where all required conditions are satisfied, including:

- authenticated user identity;
- active and approved profile;
- active Platform Administrator entitlement;
- entitlement effective dates;
- absence of suspension or revocation;
- active and unexpired Platform Administrator context, where the operation requires that context; and
- any additional condition required for the specific administrative operation.

Holding a legacy profile role such as `admin` shall not by itself provide platform-wide authority after the new entitlement model is introduced.

Where the helper is retained as `SECURITY DEFINER`, it shall:

- have an explicitly fixed safe `search_path`;
- use schema-qualified object references;
- be owned by a controlled database role;
- avoid dynamic SQL unless strictly necessary;
- expose only the minimum required result;
- avoid accepting untrusted object names;
- have EXECUTE revoked from `PUBLIC`;
- have EXECUTE granted only to the minimum approved database roles;
- be covered by positive and negative tests; and
- be documented as part of the access-control design.

Anonymous execution shall be revoked unless a separately reviewed public use case exists.

Direct execution by ordinary authenticated users shall also be restricted unless it is required for an approved RLS pattern and cannot disclose or grant authority beyond the caller’s own verified status.

Where possible, administrator checks shall be implemented through a controlled database helper used internally by policies rather than exposed as a generally callable application function.

The helper shall not bypass Row Level Security for operational project data merely because the caller is a Platform Administrator.

Platform Administrator context shall remain configuration-focused and shall not become an unrestricted operational-data bypass.

## Existing administrator checks

All existing administrator checks shall be inventoried and reconciled.

The review shall identify use of:

- `profiles.role = 'admin'`;
- `is_margdarshi_admin()`;
- hard-coded email addresses;
- JavaScript role checks;
- broad editor/admin checks;
- service-role operations;
- RPC functions;
- Storage policies;
- direct table policies; and
- any other administrator heuristic.

The system shall not maintain several inconsistent definitions of “administrator.”

The approved source of Platform Administrator authority shall be the new entitlement and active-context model.

Legacy administrator checks may remain temporarily only through a documented compatibility period and shall not independently grant broader access.

## Action-progress insert-policy defect

The confirmed `action_progress_updates` insert-policy defect shall be corrected before broader action hierarchy and verification rules are rolled out.

The corrected policy shall validate the exact action referenced by the new progress-update row.

The policy shall not approve an insert because the user is assigned to some other action or because an incorrectly correlated subquery finds an unrelated permitted record.

Before permitting an insert, the database shall verify at least:

- authenticated user;
- approved and active account;
- selected active Officer context, where implemented;
- referenced action exists;
- referenced action identifier equals the progress update’s `action_id`;
- user has an active authoritative `action_assignees` relationship for that same action, or another expressly approved progress-submission authority;
- user’s assignment is active and within effective dates;
- user has valid project or subject jurisdiction;
- action is in a workflow stage that permits progress submission;
- user has permission to submit progress for the assigned role;
- action and assignment are not cancelled, expired, suspended or revoked; and
- direct API submission satisfies the same checks as the interface.

The policy shall use `action_assignees` as the authoritative assignment source after the migration approved under Decision 14.

During the transition, any temporary compatibility logic involving `action_items.assigned_to` shall be narrowly defined, tested and removed once authoritative-assignee parity is achieved.

## Required negative tests for action progress

The security test suite shall specifically prove that:

1. a user assigned to Action A cannot submit progress to Action B;
2. a user assigned to another project cannot submit progress to the target action;
3. a user who can view the project but is not an assignee cannot submit progress;
4. a supporting assignee without progress-submission permission cannot submit progress;
5. an expired assignee cannot submit progress;
6. a revoked or suspended assignee cannot submit progress;
7. an inactive or unapproved user cannot submit progress;
8. a user cannot change the supplied `action_id` to another action through a direct API request;
9. a Platform Administrator context cannot submit operational progress without an authorised Officer assignment;
10. an external user cannot submit progress unless the external grant and action assignment expressly permit it;
11. a user cannot submit progress after the action is closed, cancelled or otherwise outside an allowed workflow stage;
12. a user cannot exploit a null, malformed or unrelated action identifier; and
13. a user cannot submit progress by relying only on legacy `assigned_to` after legacy writes are frozen.

## Required positive tests for action progress

The test suite shall also confirm that:

- an active lead assignee with permission can submit progress to the correct action;
- an authorised supporting assignee can submit progress where the workflow permits it;
- a valid external assignee can submit claimed progress where expressly authorised;
- the progress event records the correct user, Officer context, assignment and action;
- claimed progress remains distinct from verified progress;
- workflow history and notifications are created correctly; and
- authorised progress submission continues to work after page refresh, direct API validation and RLS enforcement.

## No compensating policy expansion

No other RLS policy shall be widened to compensate for the corrected progress policy.

In particular, the correction shall not introduce broad permissions based only on:

- authenticated status;
- approved profile;
- general project visibility;
- editor role;
- legacy administrator role;
- creator identity;
- issuer identity;
- office membership; or
- Platform Administrator entitlement.

Every operational permission shall remain tied to the relevant record, assignment, jurisdiction, workflow stage and approved operation.

## Deployment order

The preferred security-remediation order shall be:

1. inventory and preserve the existing Auth, function-grant and RLS configuration;
2. create reproducible migration and rollback scripts;
3. establish a security test harness;
4. correct and test the action-progress insert policy;
5. review and restrict `is_margdarshi_admin()` and its grants;
6. reconcile legacy administrator checks;
7. enable and test leaked-password protection;
8. verify login, OTP, signup, password reset and administrator workflows;
9. monitor production logs and rejected requests;
10. complete a post-deployment access review; and
11. only then proceed with broader R6.0 office, project and hierarchy enforcement.

The exact order may be adjusted where a dependency requires it, but broad R6.0 policies shall not be activated before the confirmed findings have an approved disposition and tested remediation.

## Rollback requirements

Every security change shall have a documented rollback process.

Rollback shall restore only the previous technical configuration necessary to recover service. It shall not erase audit evidence or data created during the change.

Rollback documentation shall include:

### 19. Set access-control audit retention requirements

**Question requiring confirmation:** How long must access grants, context switches, assignment changes, action assignments, automatic backfills, and manual corrections be retained, and who may view the audit history?

**Why this matters:** Time-bounded grants and context separation cannot be investigated reliably without durable records. Retention also affects storage, privacy, and support procedures.

**Available options:**

1. Append-only audit records retained for a defined statutory/departmental period.
2. Retain audit records indefinitely.
3. Retain only current state and application workflow history.

**Codex recommendation:** Use append-only audit records with a formally approved retention period and access limited to designated audit/security administrators. Record actor, active context, old/new values, reason, source, and timestamp. Do not choose a duration until Rahul confirms the governing retention rule.

**Affected areas:** future access/context audit tables, `user_assignments`, `project_user_access`, administrative changes, action workflow, migration reports, archival procedure, policies, and support access.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt append-only and tamper-evident access-control audit records. Audit history shall be preserved independently from the current operational state and shall not be overwritten when an assignment, permission, context, grant or workflow record changes.

The final disposal period shall follow the formally approved Maharashtra Government, Maharashtra PWD, MoRTH, financial, contractual, judicial and information-security record-retention requirements applicable to the relevant record category.

- security incident;
- access investigation;
- financial investigation;
- statutory inquiry;
- public-safety investigation;
- data-breach investigation; or
- another formally approved hold.

A hold shall record:

- hold identifier;
- issuing authority;
- reason;
- covered records;
- start date;
- review date;
- release authority;
- release date; and
- audit history.

Disposal shall remain blocked until the hold is formally released.

## Archival

Audit records may be transferred from operational storage to secured archival storage after the approved active-retention period.

Archival shall preserve:

- record completeness;
- timestamps;
- old and new values;
- actor and context linkage;
- referenced assignments and projects;
- batch relationships;
- integrity verification;
- legal-hold status;
- authorised search capability; and
- restoration procedures.

Archiving shall not alter the meaning or sequence of events.

## Disposal

Audit-record disposal shall occur only after:

- the approved retention period has expired;
- no legal, audit, investigation or contractual hold exists;
- the record category has been verified;
- the disposal authority has approved;
- dependency checks are complete;
- a disposal report has been generated; and
- the disposal action itself is permanently audited.

Disposal shall use an approved secure method appropriate to the storage technology.

Ordinary users and routine Platform Administrators shall not have unrestricted authority to purge audit history.

## Persons permitted to view audit history

Full access-control audit history shall be restricted to specifically authorised roles, such as:

- designated security administrator;
- designated access-control auditor;
- authorised internal auditor;
- Divisional Accountant or assigned accounts auditor for relevant financial records;
- competent vigilance or investigation authority;
- authorised Head of Office within jurisdiction;
- formally appointed system-audit personnel; and
- another officer expressly authorised for the relevant investigation or review.

Platform Administrator entitlement alone shall not automatically provide unrestricted access to every audit record.

A Platform Administrator may access technical audit information necessary to maintain the system, but access to operational, personal, financial, confidential or legally sensitive content shall require an additional authorised audit or investigation role.

## Scope of audit visibility

An authorised officer shall ordinarily see only audit records within the officer’s approved jurisdiction.

For example:

- a Division-level auditor may access relevant Division and descendant-office records;
- a Circle-level authorised auditor may access relevant Division records within that Circle;
- a Regional-level authorised auditor may access relevant Circle and Division records within that Regional Office;
- a project-specific auditor may access audit history for the assigned project;
- a security administrator may access authentication and authorization events required for security review; and
- an investigation officer may access only the records covered by the investigation authority.

Cross-office or cross-circle audit access shall require an explicit and time-bounded assignment or investigation order.

## User access to their own audit history

An ordinary user may be permitted to view a limited history of the user’s own:

- account approvals;
- active assignments;
- context switches;
- access grants;
- expiry dates;
- login or security events;
- actions assigned;
- administrative changes affecting the account; and
- access-revocation events.

The user shall not receive:

- confidential security-detection logic;
- another user’s information;
- protected investigation notes;
- administrator-only comments;
- audit evidence that would compromise an inquiry;
- system secrets; or
- information prohibited by the applicable confidentiality rule.

## Support access

Support personnel shall not receive unrestricted audit access.

Where support review is required, access shall be:

- linked to a support request;
- time-bounded;
- limited to necessary records;
- approved by an authorised person;
- read-only where possible;
- fully audited; and
- revoked after completion.

Sensitive data shall be masked where full content is not required for troubleshooting.

## External-user audit access

External contractors, consultants and agencies shall not receive internal access-control audit history.

They may receive limited information concerning their own account or grant, such as:

- access approval;
- project scope;
- permitted operations;
- expiry date;
- suspension;
- revocation;
- login-security notification; and
- their own submitted workflow events.

Internal deliberations, security rules, other organisations’ grants and administrator activity shall remain restricted.

## Audit-search and reporting controls

Audit reports shall enforce:

- active audit role;
- office or project jurisdiction;
- date-range restriction;
- event-category restriction;
- confidentiality classification;
- legal-hold restrictions;
- export permission;
- masking requirements; and
- reason for access where required.

Broad audit exports shall be prohibited by default.

Sensitive exports may require:

- approval;
- recent authentication;
- recorded purpose;
- watermarking or export identification;
- limited fields;
- secure delivery; and
- expiry or disposal confirmation.

## Audit-access logging

Every viewing, search, download or export of audit history shall itself be audited.

The audit-access event shall record:

- viewing user;
- active context;
- audit role;
- search criteria;
- record categories viewed;
- project or office scope;
- number of records returned;
- export or download status;
- stated purpose;
- approval reference, where required;
- date and time; and
- success or failure.

No person shall be able to inspect audit history invisibly.

## Integrity protection

Audit records shall be protected against unauthorised modification.

The implementation should support, as appropriate:

- restrictive insert-only interfaces;
- prohibition of routine UPDATE and DELETE;
- controlled database ownership;
- limited service-role access;
- transaction identifiers;
- chronological sequencing;
- integrity hashes or chained hashes;
- immutable archival copies;
- backup retention;
- anomaly monitoring;
- database activity logging; and
- periodic integrity verification.

Application users shall not insert arbitrary audit records directly. Audit entries shall be generated through controlled database functions, triggers, backend services or approved migration processes.

## Failure logging

Failed or rejected access-control operations shall also be recorded where technically and operationally appropriate.

This includes:

- unauthorised assignment attempt;
- invalid context switch;
- expired grant use;
- unauthorised direct API request;
- rejected external-user access;
- unauthorised audit search;
- attempt to modify audit history;
- attempt to use an inactive assignment;
- cross-project access attempt;
- cross-office access attempt;
- failed administrator action; and
- failed security migration.

Failure records shall avoid storing passwords, tokens or other secrets.

## Privacy and minimisation

Audit records shall contain enough information to investigate access decisions without unnecessarily duplicating the full contents of operational records.

Where possible, the audit shall store:

- record identifiers;
- category;
- changed fields;
- old and new values necessary for investigation;
- actor and authority information; and
- references to supporting records.

Highly sensitive attachments or full document contents shall not be copied into audit tables unless specifically required.

Personal-data access shall be limited to authorised purposes and shall follow the approved privacy and retention requirements.

## Backup and disaster recovery

Audit history shall be included in backup and disaster-recovery planning.

The system shall define:

- backup frequency;
- backup retention;
- restoration testing;
- archived-record restoration;
- legal-hold preservation;
- migration-batch restoration;
- integrity verification after recovery; and
- responsibility for recovery approval.

A rollback or restoration shall not cause duplicate, missing or reordered audit events without detection.

## Audit reports required

MARGDARSHI shall provide reports identifying:

- current and expired access grants;
- assignments created, changed or revoked;
- context switches;
- administrator-context operations;
- automatic backfills;
- manual corrections;
- cross-office grants;
- external-user grants;
- emergency access;

- action assignment and reassignment;
- rejected authorization attempts;
- audit records accessed or exported;
- audit records nearing disposal eligibility;
- records under legal hold;
- records lacking a retention class;
- records with missing actor or context;
- possible audit-sequence gaps;
- failed integrity checks;
- unauthorised audit-access attempts;
- archived records;
- disposal approvals; and
- completed disposals.

## Configuration authority

The retention schedule, retention classes, audit-viewing roles and disposal authorities shall initially be configured only through Platform Administrator context following approval by the competent departmental authority.

The Platform Administrator shall implement the approved rule but shall not independently decide the statutory retention period.

Changes to retention configuration shall record:

- previous rule;
- new rule;
- governing authority;
- reason;
- approval reference;
- effective date;
- affected record classes;
- migration impact;
- archival impact;
- disposal impact;

## Database and API enforcement

Audit-retention and audit-access rules shall be enforced in the database and backend.

The system shall prevent direct API users from:

- updating audit events;
- deleting audit events;
- viewing records outside approved audit scope;
- bypassing legal holds;
- exporting unrestricted audit history;
- changing retention dates;
- changing disposal eligibility;
- creating false administrative events; or
- hiding failed operations.

Service-level access used for audit generation or archival shall be narrowly controlled and monitored.

## Default-deny rule

Where a user does not hold an active and appropriate audit, security, investigation or jurisdictional permission, access to detailed audit history shall be denied.

General Platform Administrator, editor, project-viewer or approved-user status shall not be sufficient.

## Final retention-period confirmation

Before automatic archival or disposal is enabled, the competent authority shall approve a written retention schedule identifying:

- governing departmental or statutory rule;
- minimum retention period for each audit category;
- event from which the period is calculated;
- active-storage period;
- archival-storage period;
- legal-hold procedure;
- disposal authority;
- secure-disposal process;
- privacy requirements;
- exceptional permanent-retention categories; and
- periodic review responsibility.

Until that approval is recorded, the system shall retain the audit history without automatic deletion.

## Enforcement consistency

These rules shall apply consistently to:

- user administration;
- Officer contexts;
- Platform Administrator contexts;
- office hierarchy;
- user assignments;
- project grants;
- external-user grants;
- delegated authority;
- actions;

- attachments and downloads;
- audit search;
- reports;
- exports;
- APIs;

- backups;
- restoration; and
- secure disposal.

The approved R6.0 position shall therefore be append-only audit retention under a formally approved retention schedule, with no automatic deletion until that schedule is confirmed. Detailed audit visibility shall be limited to specifically authorised audit, security, accounts, vigilance or investigation personnel within their approved jurisdiction, and every access to the audit history shall itself be recorded.

## Migration, release, and operations decisions

### 20. Establish the database migration source of truth and context data model

**Question requiring confirmation:** Where will reviewed Supabase migrations live, and should current `user_assignments` be extended or supplemented by new context/entitlement tables?

**Why this matters:** The repository has no SQL migration history, while the live project has migrations not tracked here. R6.0 changes cannot be reproduced or rolled back safely without a versioned source of truth.

**Available options:**

1. Add repository-tracked Supabase migrations and extend `user_assignments` only where its semantics remain valid.
2. Add new normalized post/context/entitlement tables while preserving current assignments for compatibility.
3. Continue managing SQL outside the repository.

**Codex recommendation:** Establish repository-tracked, reviewable migrations before any schema change. Preserve `user_assignments` as historical officer-assignment evidence, supplement it with normalized post and context/entitlement structures where needed, and avoid destructive replacement until parity and rollback are proven.

**Affected areas:** future Supabase migration directory, `user_assignments`, post catalogue, administrator entitlement/context tables, migration history reconciliation, non-production environment, and rollback tooling.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt repository-tracked Supabase migrations as the sole reviewed source of truth for all future R6.0 database, Auth, RLS, Storage-policy, function, trigger and access-control changes.

Preserve the existing `user_assignments` table as historical evidence of officer postings and functional assignments wherever its current meaning remains valid. Supplement it with normalized post, entitlement and context structures rather than destructively replacing it.

No live-production schema change shall be treated as complete unless the corresponding reviewed migration, validation procedure and rollback instructions are committed to the repository.

- no untracked SQL change is required for the first R6.0 implementation phase.

## Enforcement rule

From the start of R6.0 implementation, no new production schema, policy, function, grant, trigger, Storage policy or access-defining data migration shall be introduced only through the Supabase dashboard or SQL Editor without a corresponding reviewed repository change.

The approved R6.0 position shall therefore be:

- repository-tracked Supabase migrations as the authoritative future source of truth;
- reconciliation and baseline capture of the existing live database;
- preservation of `user_assignments` as historical officer-assignment evidence;
- normalized post, context, entitlement and permission structures added alongside it;
- separate Platform Administrator entitlement and context records;
- additive migration and compatibility before destructive replacement; and
- retirement of legacy structures only after parity, security testing and rollback readiness are proven.

### 21. Approve existing-data migration and fallback rules

**Question requiring confirmation:** How should incomplete offices, unmapped designations, missing profile offices, open-ended project grants, users without project mappings, nullable-project records, legacy action assignees, and unresolved attachment parents be handled during migration?

**Why this matters:** Immediate strict enforcement could lock out legitimate users; permissive fallback could preserve broad access indefinitely.

**Available options:**

1. Resolve all exceptions before enforcement and deny unresolved records/users afterward.
2. Use a temporary compatibility fallback with expiry and exception reporting.
3. Preserve current broad access until every record is manually reviewed.

**Codex recommendation:** Use additive backfills and a time-limited compatibility phase. Generate exception reports, require named review for every unresolved item, preserve legacy values without treating them as authoritative, and fail closed at the end of the approved transition. Never silently infer designation or office scope.

**Affected areas:** offices, profiles, `user_assignments`, projects, `project_user_access`, correspondence, calendar, actions/assignees, attachments/documents, Storage objects, preflight reports, fallback policies, and rollback scripts.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt an additive, time-limited and fully reported compatibility migration. Existing data shall be preserved, normalized and reviewed before strict R6.0 enforcement is activated.

Temporary compatibility shall be permitted only where necessary to avoid unjustified operational lockout. Every fallback shall have:

- a named owner;
- a documented reason;
- a defined scope;
- an approval reference;
- a start date;
- an expiry date;
- an exception report;
- a remediation action;
- a review status; and
- an audit trail.

No compatibility rule shall create permanent broad access.

At the end of the approved transition period, unresolved access-defining records shall fail closed unless a competent authority approves a specific, time-bounded exception.

## General migration principles

The migration shall follow these principles:

- preserve original legacy values;
- add normalized references without immediately deleting legacy fields;
- never silently infer office, designation, jurisdiction or authority;
- classify values as confirmed, derived, inferred, ambiguous or unresolved;
- require named review for every unresolved exception;
- compare access before and after migration;
- retain rollback data;
- prevent compatibility logic from becoming a second permanent authorization model;
- resolve high-risk exceptions before lower-risk cleanup; and
- deny unresolved access after the transition expiry.

## Migration status categories

Every migrated record shall receive a migration status such as:

- confirmed;
- automatically matched;
- manually confirmed;
- inferred pending review;
- ambiguous;
- invalid;
- duplicate;
- missing dependency;
- temporarily compatible;
- blocked;
- archived;
- not applicable; or
- unresolved.

Records marked inferred, ambiguous, temporarily compatible or unresolved shall appear in exception reports until formally resolved.

## Preflight inventory

Before enforcing new R6.0 rules, the system shall inventory at least:

- offices and office hierarchy;
- profiles and account status;
- profile office values;
- designation and post values;
- user assignments;
- reporting relationships;
- project ownership and implementing offices;
- project-user access grants;
- assignment effective dates;
- external users and organisations;
- actions and action assignees;
- progress and verification records;
- correspondence and calendar scope;
- attachments and Storage objects;

- delegated authorities;

- free-text authorization fields;

- existing RLS dependencies.

The preflight report shall identify all records that would be denied under the proposed strict model.

## Incomplete office hierarchy

An office record shall not be treated as valid for inheritance unless its position in the approved hierarchy is confirmed.

The migration shall identify:

- office without office type;
- office without parent;
- invalid parent;
- circular hierarchy;
- duplicate office;
- inactive office with active users;

- office represented only through free text.

Where the relationship is ambiguous, the office shall not receive inherited access.

During compatibility, users in that office may retain only the minimum previously proven operational scope needed for current work and only through an explicit temporary grant.

## Missing profile office

A user with no valid profile office shall not automatically receive organisation-wide or project-wide access.

The migration shall determine whether office scope can be confirmed through:

- active `user_assignments`;
- official posting order;
- current project assignment;
- existing office-specific workflow;
- verified administrator record;
- approved external-user grant; or
- another reliable authority source.

Where a valid active assignment already identifies the office, the profile office may be backfilled as a compatibility or display value, but the assignment shall remain the authoritative source.

Where no office can be confirmed:

- the account may remain approved for login if necessary;
- operational project and office access shall remain restricted;
- the user shall appear in the exception report;
- the concerned Head of Office shall be asked to confirm assignment;
- no broad fallback shall be granted; and
- the user shall fail closed after the compatibility expiry.

## Unmapped designations and posts

Legacy designation text shall not be silently converted to a post based only on similar wording.

- original legacy value;
- proposed canonical post;
- confidence level;
- office types where applicable;
- functional-role implications;
- reviewer;
- authority source;
- effective date;
- ambiguity notes; and
- final status.

Exact approved matches may be automatically mapped.

Variants caused only by spelling, punctuation, abbreviation or capitalization may be normalized where the meaning is unambiguous.

Ambiguous values shall remain unmapped until reviewed.

An unmapped designation shall not independently grant supervisory, verification, approval or administrator authority.

During compatibility, the user’s confirmed office, project assignment and existing narrowly proven permissions may be retained temporarily without inferring a wider post-based authority.

Personal Assistant, Project Officer, Auditor, Divisional Accountant, specialist and similar functional roles shall not be inferred merely from permanent designation.

### 22. Assign production rollout, testing, backup, and rollback responsibility

**Question requiring confirmation:** Who owns backup, migration execution, functional and security sign-off, the maintenance window, acceptable downtime, post-deploy verification, rollback authority, and backup/audit retention?

### 23. Approve browser support and automated regression policy

**Question requiring confirmation:** Which browser/version matrix must MARGDARSHI support, and must CI run persistent JavaScript, HTML, route, accessibility, RLS, Storage, and browser regression tests?

**Why this matters:** The audit proposes cross-module and direct-API enforcement. Without a supported matrix and repeatable tests, access regressions may be detected only after deployment.

**Available options:**

1. Support current enterprise versions of Chrome and Edge first, with automated critical-path browser and database-policy tests.
2. Support all evergreen browsers with a broader automated matrix.
3. Continue manual testing only.

**Codex recommendation:** Define Chrome and Edge current stable plus one prior major version as the initial desktop baseline, add responsive viewport coverage, and automate critical authorization, route, Storage, print, and export paths before R6.0 production enforcement. Expand the matrix only after evidence of user need.

**Affected areas:** test harness, CI workflow, Auth, all routes/modules, responsive layouts, accessibility, RLS/Data API tests, Storage tests, print/CSV, and release gates.

**Rahul’s decision:**
Adopt Chrome and Microsoft Edge as the initial supported desktop-browser baseline for MARGDARSHI, covering the current stable release and one immediately preceding major version of each browser.

R6.0 production enforcement shall require persistent automated regression testing for application routes, authorization, database policies, Storage access and critical browser workflows. Manual testing shall supplement, but shall not replace, automated release-gate testing.

Support for additional browsers may be introduced later where actual departmental usage, accessibility requirements or operational evidence justifies expansion.

## Initial supported browser matrix

The initial supported desktop matrix shall include:

- Google Chrome current stable major version;
- Google Chrome immediately preceding major version;
- Microsoft Edge current stable major version; and
- Microsoft Edge immediately preceding major version.

Testing shall use the Chromium-based desktop versions ordinarily deployed on supported Windows systems.

The application shall not rely on browser-specific functionality that works only in one of the supported browsers without an approved fallback.

## Version interpretation

“Current stable plus one prior major version” shall be evaluated at the time of each production release.

When a new stable browser major version becomes generally available:

- it shall enter the supported matrix;
- the previously current version shall become the prior supported version;
- the version older than that may leave the formal test matrix after a reasonable transition period;
- release documentation shall identify the tested versions; and
- any known incompatibility shall be recorded.

Browser versions shall not be hard-coded permanently into policy documentation.

- embedded browser components with unknown engines;
- browser compatibility modes;
- browsers with JavaScript disabled;
- unsupported mobile browsers;
- browsers blocked from receiving current security updates; or
- customised enterprise browsers whose policies prevent required application functions.

Where an unsupported browser is detected, the application should display a clear and non-technical warning advising use of a supported Chrome or Edge version.

Unsupported-browser access shall not be silently granted weaker security behavior.

## Closure-review reconciliation notes

These notes reconcile terminology across the completed decisions without changing Rahul's approved policy text:

1. **Current state versus target state:** `Regional Office` is an approved R6.0 target level above Circle. The audited current schema does not yet have a `regional` office type or a Regional Office row. Likewise, `platform_admin` is the approved future entitlement/context; the audited current database still uses the legacy `profiles.role='admin'` value. Both differences are implementation and migration gaps for separately reviewed R6.0-B onward work, not descriptions of current behavior.
2. **Project-scope union:** The controlled union in Decision 6 means the scope of the one selected active Officer context plus active approved explicit project grants. It does not mean an automatic union of every substantive, additional-charge, temporary, deputation, or special-duty assignment; Decision 7 requires those Officer contexts to remain separately selectable.
3. **Assignment administration:** Decision 7's Head-of-Office or authorised-administrator authority to activate, suspend, extend, or terminate an assignment is the departmental recommendation/approval authority. Under the more specific system-governance rule in Decision 11, Platform Administrator context performs the final access-bearing system mutation until a separately reviewed `officer_admin` delegation model is implemented.
4. **Designation catalogue:** `PROJECT_OFFICER_CLASS_II` remains an official designation only where it is formally sanctioned; operational Project Officer access always comes from the separate `PROJECT_OFFICER` functional assignment. Decision 5's `JUNIOR_AUDITOR` entry is an approved addition to the normalized designation catalogue even though it was not enumerated in Decision 3's initial accounts/audit list.
5. **Approved policy versus later operational inputs:** The 25 product decisions are complete. Decisions 19, 22, and 24 deliberately make production activation conditional on later recording of the governing retention duration, named technical/security/release/support/backup/rollback owners, hosting and deployment facts, maintenance window, target dates, and final public build label. Those are rollout inputs governed by completed policy, not blank R6.0-A product decisions.
